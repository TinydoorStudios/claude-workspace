# Unit 14 — Congas ×3 × Sennheiser e604  [BLSH ch 20/21/22]

INSTRUMENT   Juan Alfredo Ríos' conga rig. Backline supplies a matched LP Classic trio on three
             Gibraltar GCS-M cradle stands, each with a tuning wrench:
                LP Classic Quinto  — 11"    (highest)
                LP Classic Conga   — 11.75" (middle)
                LP Classic Tumba   — 12.5"  (lowest)
             Sheet reads "Conga Left / Center / Right". Assigned Left=Quinto, Center=Conga,
             Right=Tumba per the standard LP trio layout — CONFIRM AT SOUNDCHECK, and if the player
             sets up mirrored, the three EQ cards swap with the drums, not with the channel numbers.
EQUIP FACT   Reference tunings for these sizes (mycongaplace / artdrum): a 10.75" quinto tunes
             roughly F3–D4 (≈175–294 Hz) and an 11.5" conga roughly Eb3–C#4 (≈156–277 Hz), with the
             tumba lower again; Poncho Sanchez tunes the trio to an inverted C major, E-G-C low to
             high. So the three drums genuinely occupy three different fundamentals — which is what
             makes slotting them possible rather than cosmetic.
MIC          Sennheiser e604 clip-on dynamic ×3 (on the cradle rims). Not ribbons, not TOUR.
             *** LOCKER FORK L2 RAISED (e604 → DPA 4099 ×3) — built as specified pending Brian. ***
SEARCHES     "conga mic technique live sound EQ frequency 200Hz 400Hz slap 5kHz clip mic tuning"
             "LP quinto conga tumba tuning fundamental pitch frequency Hz 11 inch 11.75 12.5 range"
             + the RecordingHacks e604 profile from unit 04
CAPSULE FACT e604 (RecordingHacks): proximity "+9dB @ 85Hz" at 5 cm; "High mids are emphasized,
             +3dB from 3–5kHz"; 40–18,000 Hz; wide-cardioid below 2 kHz, supercardioid above.
             Source material (AudioRecording.me / SoundOnSound): conga resonance sits "around
             200–240Hz", the fundamental "around 200Hz" with harmonics "notably at 400Hz and 700Hz",
             and "the slap is at 5kHz".
WEB SAYS     Engineer Boris Milan's approach: cut a little at 200, add a little between 300–400, and
             use a very light touch with compression because "a fast attack will diminish the slap,
             which destroys the definition of the sound". The common advice to boost ~5 kHz for
             crisp slaps is noted — and deliberately NOT followed here (see gate check).
KB SAYS      mic-library e604: scooped low-mids, voiced attack, thinner/boxier than a D-series,
             modest low end → ease off attack, mud.
VERDICT      AGREE on the mic. On the SOURCE the web and the KB don't conflict; the KB simply has no
             conga row, which is why the frequency map above comes from the external sources.
LOCKER       FORK L2 — three e604s vs three DPA 4099s (of the four owned, all free). Reason given to
             Brian in the round; building to the sheet unless he swaps.
GENRE BEND   BLSH is groove-first and danceable; the congas are a rhythmic layer under the kit, not
             a Latin feature. So they are cleaned and slotted rather than featured, and the slap is
             left to the capsule.
VENUE BEND   FSQ: each drum's ring cut runs to −6/−7. HPF set per drum, ABOVE its own fundamental
             where possible, to kill the +9 dB proximity boom that three clip mics at cradle
             distance will otherwise pile into the subs.
DRAFT BANDS  ch 20 Quinto  (≈250 Hz): HPF 180 · B4 FLAT · B3 −6 @ 700 Q2.0 · B2 −6 @ 250 Q2.2 · B1 FLAT
             ch 21 Conga   (≈200 Hz): HPF 140 · B4 FLAT · B3 −6 @ 550 Q2.0 · B2 −7 @ 200 Q2.2 · B1 FLAT
             ch 22 Tumba   (≈160 Hz): HPF 110 · B4 FLAT · B3 −5 @ 450 Q2.0 · B2 −7 @ 160 Q2.2 · B1 FLAT
GATE CHECK   No boosts on any of the three. The 5 kHz slap boost every source recommends is REFUSED
             because the e604 already emphasises 3–5 kHz by +3 dB — the slap is in the capsule — and
             at 92 % RH it arrives at the back of the plaza intact. Boosting it would be stacking a
             voiced peak three times over. B4 FLAT on all three.
             SECTION SLOTTING — the three curves are NOT near-identical, and the separation is in the
             numbers: each drum's B2 sits on ITS OWN ring (250 / 200 / 160) and each B3 sits on ITS
             OWN harmonic (700 / 550 / 450), with HPFs stepped 180 / 140 / 110 to match the
             fundamentals. Cutting all three at a shared 200 Hz would have hollowed the tumba and
             missed the quinto entirely.
QUESTIONS    Left-to-right drum order — confirm at soundcheck (in notes).
