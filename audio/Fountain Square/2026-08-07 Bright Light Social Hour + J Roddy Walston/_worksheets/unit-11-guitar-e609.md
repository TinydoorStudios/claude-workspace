# Unit 11 — Guitar cab × Sennheiser e609 Silver  [ch 13, both bands]

INSTRUMENT   BLSH: Curtis Roush, psych/space guitar — fuzz, modulation and reverb layers.
             JRW: lead guitar in a Zepp-meets-'50s band — riff-forward, midrange-driven.
EQUIP FACT   Backline ships TWO Fender combos: a '65 Twin Reverb Reissue (85 W, four 6L6, two 12"
             Jensen C-12K, blackface voicing, bright switch, huge clean headroom — Fender/MusicRadar)
             and a Blues Deluxe Reissue (40 W, three 12AX7 + two 6L6, single 12" Eminence Special
             Design, tweed-voiced, "warmest, fullest… sparkly and spanky" — Produce Like A Pro /
             Sweetwater). Ch 13 is built for the TWIN — the bright, scooped-mid, high-headroom amp —
             because it is the bigger of the two and the one a single-guitar psych rig will want.
             Confirm at load-in; if the amps swap, ch 13 and ch 14 swap their B4/B3 values with them.
MIC          Sennheiser e609 Silver, supercardioid dynamic, flat-face for close cab work.
             Not a ribbon, not TOUR.
SEARCHES     "Sennheiser e609 silver frequency response presence peak 4-5kHz dB supercardioid guitar cab spec"
             "Sennheiser e609 silver frequency response graph presence boost 3kHz 5kHz measured"
CAPSULE FACT Sennheiser official spec: 40–15,000 Hz, supercardioid. SoundMaximum's e906/e609/SM57
             comparison puts the voicing as "a 3-6,000 Hz midrange peak"; Home Studio Basics
             describes the same capsule family as having "a more-pronounced presence peak, a fuller
             HF response". Community caveat: the silver has "been accused of being a bit 'thin',
             harsh, and brittle at times" — consistent with a pronounced upper-mid peak.
WEB SAYS     Purpose-voiced for guitar cabs; the flat profile lets it hang right on the grille.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "Flat-face cab dynamic, bright upper-mid/presence ~4-5kHz, supercardioid.
             Weakness: can be edgy ~2.5-4kHz on bright amps." → ease off presence; tame harsh ~2800 Hz.
VERDICT      AGREE, with one refinement worth recording: the web puts the peak region WIDER
             (3–6 kHz) than the KB's 4–5 kHz. Same mic, broader peak — so the B4 trim is placed at
             4000, inside both readings, rather than chasing either edge. Offered as a KB refinement
             in the question round rather than silently diverging.
LOCKER       First call ("Sennheiser e609 — Guitar cab, side-address, hang over cab face"). No fork.
GENRE BEND   BLSH: the psych-mixing sources are blunt — layered "fuzz, modulation, and reverb… can
             turn murky quickly, burying clarity under low-end and midrange haze" (ClefArc /
             HotelUgly). So the haze at 900 and the mud at 300 both get cut hard, and the guitar
             keeps its 1.5–3 kHz body.
             JRW: Zepp guitar LIVES in the midrange — cutting 900 hard would gut it. The 900 cut
             drops to −4 and the channel is re-slotted against ch 14 (see unit 17).
VENUE BEND   FSQ: 300 Hz mud to −7. RH 92 %: e609 peak + a bright blackface Twin + saturated air is
             three bright things in series — this is the strongest no-boost case on the show.
DRAFT BANDS  BLSH: HPF 95 · LPF OFF · B4 −5 @ 4000 Q1.8 · B3 −6 @ 900 Q2.0 · B2 −7 @ 300 Q2.0 · B1 FLAT
             JRW:  HPF 90 · LPF OFF · B4 −4 @ 4500 Q1.8 · B3 −5 @ 900 Q2.0 · B2 −7 @ 300 Q2.0 · B1 FLAT
GATE CHECK   No boosts. The e609's 3–6 kHz peak is baked, the Twin's blackface voicing is bright on
             top of it, and 92 % RH removes any air-loss excuse — so B4 is a trim on every band.
             TWO-GUITAR SLOTTING (JRW only, paired with unit 17): ch 13 owns the TOP/jangle lane and
             ch 14 owns the MIDRANGE grind lane. Visible in the numbers — ch 13's B4 sits at 4500
             and its B3 cut is deeper (−5); ch 14's B4 sits lower at 3000 and its B3 cut is
             shallower (−3). Neither guitar is boosted in the other's lane.
