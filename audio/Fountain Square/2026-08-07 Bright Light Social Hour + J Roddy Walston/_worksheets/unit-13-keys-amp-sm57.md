# Unit 13 — Keys amp × Shure SM57  [BLSH ch 19 · JRW ch 18]

INSTRUMENT   A keyboard amplifier, mic'd. Artist-supplied on both bands (see unit 12 — the backline
             quote's Keyboard line is a stand and nothing else, $0.00) → *** TOUR flag, confirm at
             load-in, never swapped. ***
EQUIP FACT   Unknown make/model on both bands, which is itself the governing fact: nothing about the
             amp's voicing can be assumed, so the channel is built on the mic's measured curve plus
             placement, and the tonal decisions that WOULD depend on the amp are left for soundcheck.
             Placement research (Sweetwater / Dinosaur Rock Guitar / Recording Magazine): the centre
             of the cone in front of the dust cap is "the brightest, most aggressive sound with the
             most high-frequency content", the edge is darker; a practical live start is ~1" off the
             grille aimed between dust cap and edge, angled slightly off-axis — and "if you're
             experiencing harshness around 3kHz… adjust mic placement away from the cone center".
             That gives a placement lever to reach for before the EQ one.
MIC          Shure SM57. Not a ribbon. TOUR applies to the AMP, not the mic.
SEARCHES     "micing keyboard amp Rhodes Wurlitzer live SM57 speaker cone EQ 3kHz harsh live sound advice"
             + the Shure curve facts established in unit 10
CAPSULE FACT Shure published curve (user guide v3.6, 2025-A): presence peak 6–7 kHz at +5 to +7 dB,
             upward ramp from ~2 kHz, only ~+2 dB by 3 kHz, hard rolloff above ~10 kHz, ~−10 dB at
             40 Hz, and a small dip at 300–600 Hz.
WEB SAYS     The 57's presence peak is voiced for guitar amps and generally needs no correction —
             but a bright source through it, close-mic'd, gets edgy fast.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "Mid-forward workhorse, 40Hz-15kHz… thin lows, and that 6-7k peak stabs on
             bright transient sources outdoors" → trim the peak ~6000 Hz, never boost there; box
             ~400 Hz but LIGHTER than reflex.
VERDICT      AGREE — same corrected curve as unit 10, applied to a different source.
LOCKER       An honest candidate exists (Audio-Technica PRO 6L, the KB's "smoother-mid SM57
             alternative", owned) — but the win is a matter of taste rather than a concrete,
             nameable improvement for this show, and the fork rule is explicit that marginal/taste
             wins don't qualify. NOT raised, deliberately, to keep the round sharp.
GENRE BEND   BLSH ch 19: the amp is one texture among lush synth layers — it gets out of the way of
             the vocals, so the 1200 cut is deeper (−5).
             JRW ch 18: this is the amped piano and the '50s rock-and-roll BARK is the point — the
             1200 cut drops to −3 so the honk that makes it sound like a record survives.
VENUE BEND   FSQ: 350 mud to −6. The 400-ish box cut is held light for the same capsule-gate-in-
             reverse reason as unit 10 — the 57 already dips 300–600.
DRAFT BANDS  BLSH ch 19: HPF 90 · LPF OFF · B4 −4 @ 6500 Q2.0 · B3 −5 @ 1200 Q1.8 · B2 −6 @ 350 Q2.0 · B1 FLAT
             JRW  ch 18: HPF 80 · LPF OFF · B4 −4 @ 6500 Q2.0 · B3 −3 @ 1200 Q1.8 · B2 −6 @ 350 Q2.0 · B1 FLAT
GATE CHECK   No boosts. B4 sits on the measured 6–7 kHz peak as a trim; at 92 % RH that peak reaches
             the back of the plaza intact, and an amped electric-piano patch is already bright, so
             adding there would be indefensible twice over.
             Where this channel overlaps the keys DI (unit 12), the DI owns the body and sustain and
             the amp owns the bark and grit — the DI's B3 cut sits at 1500 and the amp's at 1200, so
             they are not cutting the same hole, and neither is boosted in the other's lane.
