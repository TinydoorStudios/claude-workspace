# Unit 05 — Hi-hat × Shure SM81  [ch 5, both bands]

INSTRUMENT   Hi-hat on a DW9000 three-legged stand (backline). CYMBALS ARE BAND-SUPPLIED — the
             quote states "ARTIST RIDER DOES NOT REQUEST CYMBALS", so the actual hats are unknown
             and may differ between the two bands. Built conservatively for that reason.
EQUIP FACT   The three-leg (non-double-braced-two-leg) stand and a band's own hats mean nothing is
             known about weight or pitch — which is exactly why a flat mic is the right tool and
             why no HF shaping is baked in ahead of soundcheck.
MIC          Shure SM81, cardioid SDC. Not a ribbon, not TOUR. Switches: LF filter assumed FLAT,
             pad assumed OUT — fallback in mic_notes.
SEARCHES     "Shure SM81 frequency response flat hi-hat overhead spec 10kHz rise dB SPL"
             "Shure SM81 frequency response curve slight rise 6-10kHz proximity flat gearspace hi-hat EQ live sound"
             → RecordingHacks SM81 profile (fetched) + soundref / Everything Audio Network
CAPSULE FACT RecordingHacks/Shure: 20 Hz–20 kHz, "flat response curve", max SPL 146 dB, self-noise
             16.0 dB(A), selectable LF response flat / 6 / 18 dB-per-octave, 0/10 dB attenuator.
             soundref: "extremely flat and wide frequency response especially above 5 kHz… probably
             one of the flattest-sounding cardioids ever made… really flat down to around 200 Hz."
WEB SAYS     Accurate rather than flattering — it takes EQ well precisely because it adds nothing.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "Ruler-flat SDC, clean detailed highs with no harsh fizz, flat/6/18dB HPF.
             Accurate on hat/OH/acoustic — takes EQ well. Weakness: needs care on cymbal wash, no
             built-in flattery." → apply template as-is (flat/honest).
VERDICT      AGREE — both sides say the same thing: no baked peak, no baked scoop.
LOCKER       First call ("Shure SM81 — Overheads, hi-hat, acoustic instruments"). No fork.
GENRE BEND   BLSH (danceable psych-groove): the hat rides the time and the dance floor needs it
             crisp and present — minimum trim up top (−2).
             JRW (Zepp/'50s): the hat is looser and washier and sits behind the kit; it gets pushed
             back with a deeper top trim (−4) and a slightly higher HPF.
VENUE BEND   HPF 500 per the hat rule — everything below is bleed, and outdoors there is no room
             to lose. RH 92 %: the genre-reflex 8–10 kHz shimmer lift is INVERTED into a cut, per
             the fetched-humidity rule — saturated air delivers the top intact and open hats
             through an A15 array turn spitty.
DRAFT BANDS  BLSH: HPF 500 · LPF OFF · B4 −2 @ 9000 Q2.0 · B3 −6 @ 800 Q2.0 · B2 FLAT · B1 FLAT
             JRW:  HPF 550 · LPF OFF · B4 −4 @ 9000 Q2.0 · B3 −6 @ 800 Q2.0 · B2 FLAT · B1 FLAT
GATE CHECK   No boosts anywhere. Normally a flat capsule is the one place an air lift is defensible
             (nothing baked to stack on) — but the 94 % RH forecast removes the reason for it, so
             the same band becomes a cut instead. That inversion is the whole point of fetching the
             weather. B3 −6 @ 800 clears snare-bleed clank, which the cardioid pattern won't reject.
