# Unit 17 — Guitar 2 cab × Electro-Voice N/D 408  [JRW ch 14]

INSTRUMENT   Second guitar in the J Roddy band — the rhythm/grind side of a two-guitar Zepp-meets-
             '50s front line.
EQUIP FACT   Built for the Fender Blues Deluxe Reissue: 40 W, three 12AX7 + two 6L6, one 12"
             Eminence Special Design, TWEED-voiced. Reviewers put it as "the warmest, fullest, most
             tonally balanced, sparkly and spanky amp", a throwback to Fender's '90s Tweed Series
             tribute to the 1950s amps (Produce Like A Pro / Sweetwater / MusicRadar). That is the
             deliberate opposite of ch 13's blackface Twin — warm and mid-forward against bright and
             scooped — and it is the whole basis of the two-guitar slot. If the amps land the other
             way round at load-in, ch 13 and ch 14 swap curves.
MIC          Electro-Voice N/D 408, supercardioid N/DYM neodymium dynamic on a pivoting yoke.
             Vintage USA, discontinued. Not a ribbon, not TOUR.
SEARCHES     "Electro-Voice N/D408 frequency response curve presence peak dB supercardioid tom guitar cab specification"
             (EV N/D 408B data sheet + Gearspace product page)
CAPSULE FACT EV published spec: frequency response 30–22,000 Hz; the N/DYM magnetic structure
             "provides 6-dB more output sensitivity over conventional designs" with "a more uniform
             magnetic field [that] lowers distortion during peak sound pressure levels"; the uniform
             supercardioid pattern "insures superior gain-before-feedback in live applications".
             Gearspace: "popular on guitar cabs, snares, and toms."
WEB SAYS     Aggressive and forward in the upper mids relative to a 57, with a real proximity lift;
             the small pivoting head gets it where a 421 won't fit.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "Supercardioid N/DYM neodymium dynamic, 30Hz-22kHz close / 60Hz-22kHz far,
             brighter and more aggressive upper mids than an SM57, deliberate proximity lift, high
             SPL… Weakness: that upper-mid bite gets spiky on bright sources; close working distance
             piles low-mids; discontinued." → ease off presence, attack; tame box ~400 Hz.
VERDICT      AGREE — EV's own "6 dB more output" and the KB's "brighter and more aggressive upper
             mids" describe the same forward character; the two split ranges (close vs far) explain
             the proximity behaviour the KB flags.
LOCKER       The MD 421-U is the obvious alternative and the KB even calls the 408 "the small-
             footprint 421 alternative" — but the 408 was spec'd deliberately for this slot and the
             421's low-mid girth would fight the tweed amp's already-fat midrange rather than help
             it. Not a concrete win for THIS show → no fork raised.
GENRE BEND   Zepp guitar lives in the midrange, so this channel is allowed to keep its 900 Hz region
             almost intact (−3 only) — that is what stops the pair sounding like two thin guitars.
VENUE BEND   FSQ: the 250 Hz cut runs to −6, aimed squarely at the low-mid pile-up the KB warns
             about at close working distance. RH 92 %: the baked upper-mid bite arrives whole, so
             the 3 kHz move is a trim.
DRAFT BANDS  HPF 100 · LPF OFF · B4 −5 @ 3000 Q1.8 · B3 −3 @ 900 Q2.0 · B2 −6 @ 250 Q2.0 · B1 FLAT
GATE CHECK   No boosts. The 408's aggressive upper mids are baked and it is sitting on a mid-forward
             tweed amp — two voicings in series — so B4 is a −5 trim, the deepest presence trim on
             either band's guitars.
             TWO-GUITAR SLOTTING (with unit 11), visible in the values rather than asserted:
                ch 13 (Twin, bright/scooped)  B4 −4 @ 4500 · B3 −5 @ 900 → owns the TOP/jangle lane
                ch 14 (Blues Deluxe, warm)    B4 −5 @ 3000 · B3 −3 @ 900 → owns the MIDRANGE lane
             Different B4 centres (4500 vs 3000) and opposite-weighted B3 cuts (−5 vs −3). Neither
             guitar is boosted anywhere, so the separation is achieved entirely by where each one is
             cut — which is the only way it can be done under a cuts-first rule.
