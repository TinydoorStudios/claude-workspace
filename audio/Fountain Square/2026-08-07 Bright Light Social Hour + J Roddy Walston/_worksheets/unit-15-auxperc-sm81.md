# Unit 15 — Aux percussion × Shure SM81  [BLSH ch 23]

INSTRUMENT   Hand percussion on a Gibraltar 7615 large accessory table (24"×24") with table-top legs
             — tambourine, shakers, blocks, bells, whatever Ríos has laid out. One mic over a table
             of small, bright, transient sources.
EQUIP FACT   The 24"×24" table is the governing equipment fact: it is a large flat resonant surface
             that the mic is pointed straight at, so it contributes its own low-mid box tone
             (~1 kHz region) on every hit — which is what the B3 cut is actually for. It also means
             the mic is close to a hard reflector, so bleed rejection matters more than reach.
MIC          Shure SM81, cardioid SDC (the second of the two on this show — the other is the hat).
             Not a ribbon, not TOUR. LF filter assumed FLAT, pad assumed OUT — fallback in mic_notes.
SEARCHES     shared with unit 05 (SM81 spec + curve description)
CAPSULE FACT RecordingHacks/Shure: 20 Hz–20 kHz, flat response curve, 146 dB max SPL, 16.0 dB(A)
             self-noise, selectable flat / 6 / 18 dB-per-octave LF rolloff, 0/10 dB attenuator.
             soundref: "extremely flat and wide… especially above 5 kHz… one of the flattest-sounding
             cardioids ever made."
WEB SAYS     Accurate, unflattering, takes EQ well — everything you hear is the source and the room.
KB SAYS      mic-library: ruler-flat, clean detailed highs with no harsh fizz, needs care on cymbal
             wash, no built-in flattery → apply template as-is.
VERDICT      AGREE.
LOCKER       First call for acoustic/percussion detail. The only other flat SDC-class option is the
             second SM81 — which IS this mic. No fork.
GENRE BEND   BLSH: percussion is texture inside a groove, riding under the kit rather than featured.
             Clean and controlled, no lift.
VENUE BEND   HPF 250 — table percussion has no useful content below that, and a high pass here is
             the cheapest rejection available against kick and conga bleed arriving off the table.
             RH 92 %: a tambourine's 8–12 kHz content and a shaker's whole voice are HF — saturated
             air delivers all of it, and through an A15 array that is an ice pick. So the top gets
             CUT, which is the exact inverse of the usual "add air to hand percussion" instinct.
DRAFT BANDS  HPF 250 · LPF OFF · B4 −4 @ 9000 Q2.0 · B3 −5 @ 1000 Q2.0 · B2 FLAT · B1 FLAT
GATE CHECK   No boosts. The SM81 bakes in nothing, so for once there is no capsule peak to argue
             against a lift — the reason there is no lift is entirely the fetched humidity. Worth
             stating plainly because it is the same logic as unit 05 and the two channels must not
             be allowed to drift apart: flat mic + saturated air = trim, not add.
             B3 −5 @ 1000 is the accessory table's own box tone, per the equipment note above.
QUESTIONS    none
