# Unit 07 — 18" floor tom × Audix D4  [ch 8, both bands]

INSTRUMENT   DW Collectors Series floor tom, 16"D × 18"W, on three DW legs (backline). The biggest
             drum on the kit after the kick.
EQUIP FACT   An 18" floor tom's fundamental sits around 70–80 Hz — which lands almost exactly on the
             D4's documented low-frequency rolloff knee. That coincidence is what makes the low bell
             on this channel legitimate rather than lazy.
MIC          Audix D4, hypercardioid dynamic. Not a ribbon, not TOUR.
SEARCHES     "Audix D4 frequency response rolloff peaks" → RecordingHacks D4 profile (fetched)
CAPSULE FACT RecordingHacks, reading Audix's CURRENT chart: 40–18,000 Hz, hypercardioid, 144 dB max
             SPL, "+6dB at 5kHz" with "a secondary peak above 10kHz", plus "a low-frequency rolloff
             below 70Hz". The page also notes the older (c.2001) spec showed a different curve
             "peaking at +9dB at 10kHz" — the current chart is the one that governs.
WEB SAYS     Sold as a low-frequency specialist, but the measured chart shows it does NOT lack
             upper-mid attack and does NOT reach into the 30s.
KB SAYS      mic-library carries the same current-chart reading verbatim (row corrected 2026-07-30
             on Brian's call during the Repertoire build, replacing an older row that would have put
             a click boost straight onto a baked peak) → "trim the peak ~5000Hz, never boost there;
             restore weight ~80Hz rather than expecting sub reach."
VERDICT      AGREE — and notably the KB row was corrected FROM the same source, so this is a
             cross-check rather than an independent confirmation. Called out so it isn't oversold.
LOCKER       First call ("Audix D4 — Floor tom, more low-end extension than D2"). No fork.
GENRE BEND   BLSH: floor tom punctuates grooves — controlled, cut mud harder.
             JRW: Zepp floor tom is enormous and is allowed to ring; shallower mud cut, bigger low.
VENUE BEND   FSQ: 400 Hz boom cut deep (−7/−6). RH 92 % makes the 5 kHz peak and the >10 kHz
             secondary peak both arrive intact → both get trimmed by the one B4 move at 5 k.
DRAFT BANDS  BLSH: HPF 45 · LPF OFF · B4 −4 @ 5000 Q1.6 · B3 −7 @ 400 Q1.8 · B2 FLAT · B1 +3 @ 80 Q1.2
             JRW:  HPF 40 · LPF OFF · B4 −3 @ 5000 Q1.6 · B3 −6 @ 400 Q1.8 · B2 FLAT · B1 +4 @ 80 Q1.2
GATE CHECK   The B4 move is a TRIM, not a boost — the "click" a floor tom is usually given at 5 kHz
             is already +6 dB in this capsule. This is the exact trap the KB row was rewritten to
             stop, and it is being honoured.
             The +3/+4 @ 80 PASSES the gate: the documented rolloff starts below 70 Hz, so 80 Hz sits
             on the shoulder where the mic is giving up, and the drum's fundamental is right there.
             The KB's own instruction is "restore weight ~80Hz rather than expecting sub reach" —
             which is what this band does. Nothing is asked of the mic below 70; the KS21 arch owns
             that.
QUESTIONS    none
