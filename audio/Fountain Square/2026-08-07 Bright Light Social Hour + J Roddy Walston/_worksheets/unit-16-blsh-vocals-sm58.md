CAPSULE FACT RecordingHacks/Shure: 50–15,000 Hz, cardioid, "frequency response tailored for vocals,
             with brightened midrange and bass rolloff"; the same page notes the Beta 58 has
             "extended low end and high end compared to SM58" and "a 4dB hotter output than the SM58".
WEB SAYS     The workhorse — forgiving, consistent, limited air, gets muddy worked close.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "Cardioid vocal dynamic, presence ~4-6kHz, gentle low rolloff, proximity.
             Tough, consistent. Weakness: muddy if close, limited air, upper-mid spit." → tame box
             ~500 Hz.
VERDICT      AGREE — brightened midrange = the 4–6 kHz presence region; bass rolloff = the gentle
             low rolloff. No conflict.
LOCKER       FORK L3 — raised with the availability caveat stated honestly as sentence three.
GENRE BEND   BLSH vocals are not a belted lead over a band — press describes "surreal, dreamlike…
             take me right to bed vocals" (Denver Post) and the EPK's own "cozmic soul". They sit IN
             the mix, wrapped in reverb, three parts blending. So the cuts are moderate and the goal
             is separation between the three voices rather than pushing any one forward.
VENUE BEND   FSQ + three open mics in front of wedges: HPFs run high and cuts-only is doubly
             justified. RH 92 % climbing across the set → the de-essers are DYNAMIC, not static,
             because a top-end value dialled at 7 pm is wrong by 10 pm as the air keeps closing up.
DRAFT BANDS  ch 25 Curtis: HPF  90 · B4 −3 @ 8000 Q2.5 +DEQ · B3 −4 @ 1600 Q1.8 · B2 −6 @ 400 Q2.0 · B1 FLAT
             ch 26 Jackie: HPF  85 · B4 −3 @ 7500 Q2.5 +DEQ · B3 −4 @ 1200 Q1.8 · B2 −6 @ 350 Q2.0 · B1 FLAT
             ch 27 Mia:    HPF 110 · B4 −3 @ 9000 Q2.5 +DEQ · B3 −4 @ 2200 Q1.8 · B2 −7 @ 450 Q2.0 · B1 FLAT
             DEQ on all three: thr −18 dB, atk 3 ms, rel 120 ms.
GATE CHECK   ZERO boosts — vocals are cuts-only, every genre, no exceptions, and none was drafted.
             The 4–6 kHz presence is baked into the capsule and 92 % RH delivers it, so the B4 moves
             are trims placed above it.
             VOICE-TYPE SLOTTING (the separation is in the numbers, not the prose): two male-register
             voices and one upper-register voice, so all three axes are staggered —
                HPF   90 / 85 / 110  (male 60–100, female 80–120 per the live-HPF anchors)
                B4  8000 / 7500 / 9000 — three different de-ess centres
                B3  1600 / 1200 / 2200 — three DIFFERENT nasal lanes, so the harmonies don't mask
                B2   400 /  350 /  450 — box cut tracks each voice's chest register
             Cutting all three at a shared 1.5 kHz would have made three good mics sound like one.
