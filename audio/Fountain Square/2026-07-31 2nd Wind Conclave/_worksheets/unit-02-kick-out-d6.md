# Unit 02 — Kick out × Audix D6  (ch 2)

INSTRUMENT   Kick drum, outside the resonant head / at the port. Bottom half of the two-mic
             kick with ch 1. R&B dance set — this mic is the weight under the groove.

MIC          Audix D6 — cardioid dynamic, no 48V, Short stand. The most pre-voiced kick mic in
             the locker. KB tendency: "pre-scooped smiley voicing… thump+click baked in; needs
             almost no EQ → ease off attack, boom, box, mud."

SEARCHES     1. `Audix D6 kick drum frequency response scoop 600Hz boost 63Hz 4kHz measurement
                review`
             2. Cross-read of the returned RecordingHacks D6 page, Mix Online D6X real-world
                review and Tape Op's D6 professional test for the response numbers.

CAPSULE FACT **Mid scoop of ~15–17 dB centred 600–800 Hz; LF peak at ~60 Hz (~+5 dB around
             40 Hz); HF peaks at ~4 kHz and ~10 kHz, gentle rise from 4.5 k out to ~15 k, then
             rolls off** — RecordingHacks/Mix/Tape Op agree; the 90 Hz–600 Hz band is
             deliberately attenuated to kill boom. There is also a smaller baked peak just
             above 1 kHz that Audix voices in "for clarity and definition."

WEB SAYS     The D6 is a finished kick sound out of the box — "pre-EQ'd," smiley curve, thump
             plus beater click already in the capsule. The reviewers' recurring warning is the
             flip side: it is a one-trick mic with no midrange body, and EQ'ing it like a flat
             mic double-dips moves the capsule already made.

KB SAYS      mic-library: "peaks ~63Hz and ~5kHz, deep mid scoop ~600Hz (−15dB). Thump+click
             baked in; needs almost no EQ. Weakness: one-trick, no midrange body."

VERDICT      **AGREE.** Same curve, same conclusion. One refinement worth capturing: the web
             sources put the scoop's centre at **700–800 Hz at about −17 dB**, slightly lower
             and deeper than the KB's "~600 Hz (−15 dB)", and the KB has no entry for the
             **baked peak just above 1 kHz**. Not a conflict — a more precise number and a
             missing fact. → offered as a KB update in the question round.

LOCKER       **Considered and not raised.** The obvious alternative for the low-end lane is the
             Earthworks DM6 SeisMic (DK-6, unassigned this show) — flat and uncoloured where
             the D6's −17 dB scoop is permanent. But it loses on this venue: a condenser sitting
             outside the resonant head in 16 mph gusts is a wind problem, and it trades a
             finished sound for soundcheck time on a fast conclave turnaround. Tie goes to the
             specified mic. No fork to Brian.

GENRE BEND   R&B/funk: the D6's baked smiley IS the sound this genre wants — no argument with
             the capsule. Artist layer: with a programmed kick in the Track channel (ch 16),
             the live kick's 1–1.5 kHz "definition" peak starts competing with four vocals for
             intelligibility, so that baked peak comes down rather than staying.

VENUE BEND   FSQ: 8× KS21 per side already own everything below ~45 Hz, so the mic doesn't need
             to deliver it, and an HPF up at 40 buys wind and stage-rumble immunity in the gusts.
             No room gain to fill in mids — but the correct answer to that is *not* boosting the
             D6's scoop back, it's letting ch 1 carry mids.

DRAFT BANDS  HPF 40 · LPF 8000
             B4  −4 @ 4500  Q 1.5  BELL   trim the baked click — the 91A owns the top lane
             B3  −4 @ 1200  Q 1.8  BELL   trim the baked "definition" peak out of the vocal path
             B2  FLAT
             B1  FLAT

GATE CHECK   **No boosts on this channel — deliberately.** Every place a kick would normally be
             boosted, this capsule already boosted: +5 dB at 40–60 Hz means a low boost would
             stack, and the 4 k / 10 k rise means a click boost would stack. Both moves are
             therefore trims or nothing. And the gate in reverse: the 600–800 Hz region is
             already down 15–17 dB in the capsule, so **there is no box/mud cut on this
             channel** even though the FSQ override would normally push one to −8. Cutting
             there would be double-dipping a hole that already exists.
             **Two-mic lane split (with ch 1, Beta 91A):**
             · BOTTOM (50–90 Hz): **D6 owns it** — baked 60 Hz peak, outside/port position.
               ch 1 is HPF'd 60 and cut −6 @ 200 so it can't stack.
             · TOP (3–6 k): **91A owns it** — the D6's baked 4 k click is trimmed −4 here and
               the LPF at 8 k removes its 10 k peak entirely.
             · No boost on both mics in the same zone, top or bottom. Confirmed.
             · Mono-sum check at soundcheck; if thinner than either alone, flip polarity on
               this channel (the outside mic is the one to flip).

QUESTIONS    KB-update offer only (see VERDICT) — carried into the round, not blocking.
