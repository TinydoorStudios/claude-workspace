# Unit 15 — Toys / aux percussion × Shure SM81  (ch 20)

INSTRUMENT   "Toys" — the percussionist's tray: tambourine, shakers, cowbell, woodblock,
             triangle, whatever the arrangement calls for. One mic over a collection of small,
             bright, transient-heavy sources with wildly different levels. In an R&B/funk set
             these are the details that make a groove feel like a record.

MIC          Shure SM81 — cardioid SDC, 48V, boom stand over the tray. KB tendency: "ruler-flat
             SDC, clean detailed highs with no harsh fizz, flat/6/18 dB HPF. Accurate on
             hat/OH/acoustic — takes EQ well. Weakness: needs care on cymbal wash, no built-in
             flattery. → **apply template as-is (flat/honest)**."
             **Switchable hardware: three-position low-frequency switch. ASSUMED FLAT.** The
             desk owns the HPF so it can be moved during the set. Fallback if the switch is
             found at the 18 dB position: the mic is already cutting hard below 80 Hz, so the
             desk HPF at 250 is still the operative filter and nothing changes.

SEARCHES     1. `Shure SM81 frequency response flat specification high pass filter 6dB 18dB
                rolloff percussion overhead spec sheet`
             2. Cross-read of the returned Shure SM81 user guide / spec sheet, RecordingHacks
                and the Sweetwater spec PDF.

CAPSULE FACT **20 Hz – 20 kHz with a genuinely flat response curve**, plus a three-position
             low-frequency switch: **flat · 6 dB/octave below 100 Hz · 18 dB/octave below 80 Hz**
             (Shure spec). Shure's own description of the two filters is the useful part — the
             6 dB setting exists to compensate proximity or dull/muddy sources, the 18 dB
             setting to kill floor rumble and room noise.

WEB SAYS     Universal consensus on the SM81: sonic accuracy is the whole point. It flatters
             nothing and it adds nothing, which is why it's the standard for hat, overheads and
             acoustic sources. The recurring live caution is the flip side — it reports cymbal
             wash and room noise as honestly as it reports the source.

KB SAYS      As above: flat/honest, takes EQ well, needs care on wash, no built-in flattery.

VERDICT      **AGREE.** Both sides say "flat, honest, no colour." Nothing to escalate.

LOCKER       **Silent pass.** The SM81 is the locker's stated call for exactly this ("overheads,
             hi-hat, acoustic instruments") and it's the right mic for a tray of mixed small
             percussion precisely because it doesn't impose a voicing on sources that all sound
             different. No fork.

GENRE BEND   R&B/funk: toys are the top-end movement. They need to be *present* and *controlled*
             — a tambourine that's too bright on an outdoor PA is the most fatiguing sound at a
             four-hour block party. Artist layer: with programmed percussion already in the
             Track channel, the live toys layer on top of it, so they need to be narrow.

VENUE BEND   FSQ outdoor, gusts to 16 mph: an open boom mic pointing at a tray is a wash and
             wind collector with essentially nothing useful below the sources' own pitch. HPF
             at 250, no hesitation. And with no room to soften anything, the bright end gets
             trimmed rather than left alone.

DRAFT BANDS  HPF 250 · LPF 18000
             B4  −3 @ 8000  Q 2.0  BELL   tambourine/shaker sizzle, tamed for an outdoor PA
             B3  −6 @ 1000  Q 2.0  BELL   cowbell/woodblock honk — clears the four vocals
             B2  −5 @ 400   Q 2.0  BELL   tray and stand box, plus stage wash
             B1  FLAT

GATE CHECK   **No boosts on this channel at all — and that's the capsule doing its job.** A flat
             mic on sources that are already bright has nothing missing to add. Every generic
             percussion chart would put a high boost here; the honest answer is that a
             tambourine through an SM81 arrives with more top than an outdoor PA wants, so the
             top gets a trim instead. The KB's own tendency line for this mic is "apply template
             as-is" — the template for bright percussion is subtractive.
             **Humidity check:** RH goes 50% → 77% across the set, so HF carries *better* as the
             night goes on. A −3 at 8 k set at 6 pm will feel about right at 11 pm; a +3 would
             have been unbearable.
             **Against the other bright channels:** hat (unit 04) lifts 10 k, overheads (unit 07)
             trim 9 k, bongos (unit 14) trim 6 k, toys trim 8 k. Four bright channels, four
             different treatments — nothing stacked, nothing duplicated.

QUESTIONS    None.
