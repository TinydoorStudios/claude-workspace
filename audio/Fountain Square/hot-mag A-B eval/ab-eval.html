FSQ · Q225 · 2026-07-11 · Evaluated 2026-07-08 · All six deliverables in each folder reviewed in full.
Verdict: hot-mag 3 (Fable 5) is the stronger packet. The EQ shapes are broadly similar — both models followed the house rules without a single guardrail violation — but Fable 5 grounded every channel in named, quantitative source research and then actually executed the principles its research surfaced. Opus 4.8 researched the unusual sources well, leaned on the KB for the familiar ones, and stated several principles it didn't carry into the numbers. Two Opus moves would likely get corrected at soundcheck; nothing in the Fable set reads as a mistake.

1. What's identical — the pipeline held for both

Both runs honored every hard rule: OH as a stereo pair on fader 9 with fader 10 (SNARE PL8 return) untouched, cuts-first, whole-dB, no high-shelf, vocals cuts-only with the DEQ on the Beta 58A's 4k peak, FSQ deeper-cut rule (−6 to −9) applied, the template-override warning on faders 25/26 called out, ribbon/48V handling correct, the full Seventh Heaven Pro reverb section shipped, same five monitor mixes, same 17 active channels, same patch. The .ses files are byte-identical in size with matching apply scripts — only the EQ payload differs. The A/B is clean: everything that differs is a decision, not a process error.

6. Why the differences happened — model behavior

Research budget allocation. Both models were told the same thing: web-research every instrument × mic unit, KB as cross-check. Opus 4.8 triaged — it spent web research on the three units that felt unusual (Pro 35 clip-ons, banjo DI, NOLA brass as a topic) and treated the KB as sufficient for mics it "knows" (57, 58A, i5, D6, D2/D4). That's an efficiency instinct that reads as reasonable but breaks the pipeline's premise: the KB is the floor, not the answer. Fable 5 ran the literal instruction — eleven units, each with a named source and a number attached — and that quantitative grounding is directly what produced the i5 trim-not-boost, the D6 de-stack, the Beta 27 fizz trim, and the corrected banjo frequencies.

Stating principles vs. executing them. Opus's research summary contains "frequency-slot the three horns" — and its three horn EQs are nearly identical. It also wrote "keep its lows tucked under the D6" on the 91A — then boosted +3 @ 70 anyway. This is the pattern of a model that reasons correctly at the summary level but doesn't audit its own numbers against its own stated principles. Fable's channel values trace back to its research claims one-for-one; the horn section note even names the lane each horn owns so the execution is checkable.

Fresh data vs. plausible priors. The weather step is the cleanest demonstration: Opus pattern-matched "mid-July = warm/dry = extra HF loss" — plausible, unverified, and wrong for this date (74% RH forecast). Fable fetched the forecast and reasoned from the actual numbers, reaching the opposite HF conclusion. Same pipeline text, different instinct about what needs checking. (The skill's weather paragraph only describes the dry case — Opus followed the letter; Fable generalized correctly. The pipeline fix addresses this.)

Anchoring outputs to verifiable references. Fable anchors reverb values to factory presets, flags the 91A contour switch state, and quantifies compressor settings. Opus writes plausible bare values. Anchored output is auditable at the desk; bare output has to be trusted.

Where Opus was equal or arguably ahead. Artist research (equal). Hi-hat and rack tom EQ (functionally identical). Opus's kick attack at 3.5k vs Fable's 5k — both defensible for a 91A. Opus's deeper hat edge cut (−7 vs −6) leans harder into the FSQ deep-cut rule. And Opus's open FLAG on the e609 hat mic was a legitimate instinct — Fable simply resolved the same concern through the question round instead of leaving it open, which is what the pipeline prefers.
