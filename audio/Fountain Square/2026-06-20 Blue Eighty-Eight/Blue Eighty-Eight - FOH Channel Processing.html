WATCHPOINTS
⚠ PHANTOM POWER — Ch 18 & 19 (AT Pro 35)
Both sax channels require 48V phantom. The AT Pro 35 runs through an AT8538 inline phantom power module — no phantom means no signal, not degraded signal. Confirm phantom is on before soundcheck. All other channels are passive DIs or dynamic mics; no phantom required elsewhere on this show.
⚠ AT PRO 35 MIC HPF SWITCH — Ch 18 & 19
The AT Pro 35 has a built-in 80 Hz HPF switch at the capsule. Engage it on both mics before soundcheck. The console HPF sits above it at 150 Hz — the combination is intentional and handles outdoor wind and stage handling noise. Gate both channels; key slap between phrases is constant on clip-on sax mics.
E-KIT STEREO PAIR — Ch 9 & 10
E-kick is folded into the stereo L/R output from the drum module. HPF stays at 40 Hz on both channels — anything higher eats kick body. Link Ch 9 and Ch 10 as a stereo pair on the Q225. Match gain before any EQ. Polarity check both channels against each other in mono. Kit image should sit naturally in the center field before soundcheck opens.
MD DOUBLING — Ch 12, 17, 30
The Music Director is on three channels: Ch 12 (Bass Keys), Ch 17 (Keys), and Ch 30 (vocal). This is the most routing-complex position on the stage. Coordinate with the M32 operator before soundcheck — the MD monitor mix needs to account for all three sources. The vocal channel (Ch 30) is the primary FOH presence.
FSQ OUTDOOR EQ APPROACH
All channels on this show follow the FSQ outdoor EQ rule: cuts over boosts throughout. The L-Acoustics A15/KS21 rig has clean high-end and deep sub extension — it doesn't need help from channel EQ. Vocals are cuts-only with no exceptions. The primary risk is gain-before-feedback on open mics in a downtown ambient noise floor, not room reflections.
TWO GUITARS — Ch 13 & 14 CARVE
Both guitar channels use the same mic (e609). Starting values are intentionally offset — Ch 13 cuts at 3 kHz / 300 Hz, Ch 14 cuts at 3.5 kHz / 350 Hz — to create natural frequency separation without forcing it. If they compete in the mix, widen the separation before reaching for level. Check on the guitar bus together rather than independently.
MONITOR-ONLY — Ch 23 & 24
Click (Ch 23) and Guide (Ch 24) are routed to monitors only. Confirm no FOH path is open on either channel. These feeds originate from playback — confirm routing at patch and verify at the M32 before soundcheck.

Ch 18 — Sax 1  ·  AT Pro 35  ⚠ 48V
SectionParameter / ValueDetails
HPF150 Hz @ 24 dB/Oct* HPF higher than indoor equivalent — outdoor wind noise and ambient floor justify the offset.
Band 4; High Shelf-3 dB @ 10 kHzQ 0.71; Shelf — The Pro 35 response falls off above 15 kHz anyway. Rolling off at 10 kHz removes harshness in the cap rolloff region without losing useful content. Do not try to boost air on this mic — it isn't there.
Band 3; Upper-Mid Bell-3 dB @ 1.2 kHzQ 2.5; narrow — Clip-on key noise. Mechanical transients from pad mechanisms sit in the 1–1.5 kHz range and bleed constantly between phrases. Narrow cut minimises impact on tone.
Band 2; Lower-Mid Bell-3 dB @ 350 HzQ 2.0 — Boxy proximity buildup from the clip-on position near the bell. Close placement exaggerates this range.
Band 1; LowFLATConsole HPF at 150 Hz handles this plus the mic built-in 80 Hz HPF. Don't stack more low cuts.
Quick Summary AT Pro 35 clip-on on sax. Two mandatory checks before soundcheck: (1) 48V phantom is on — the Pro 35 runs off the AT8538 inline module and requires phantom; no phantom means no signal; (2) the mic built-in 80 Hz HPF switch is engaged at the capsule. The 1.2 kHz narrow cut is the most important move on this mic — key slap between phrases is the constant enemy on clip-on sax. Gate strongly recommended; set threshold so it closes cleanly between phrases without chopping held notes.
