# Unit 16 — Toys / aux percussion × Shure SM81 · CH 19

INSTRUMENT   Auxiliary percussion tray — tambourine, shaker, bell tree, woodblock, triangle class
             of instruments. This is the ONLY hand percussion left on the list: rev 1 carried
             congas ×2 and bongos, and all three are gone this week, which is the biggest single
             change to the show's midrange picture. Role in THIS mix: the sixteenth-note sparkle
             that makes an R&B/funk groove feel busy without adding another drum.
             Renumbered from rev 1's fader 20 to fader 19; same mic.

MIC          Shure SM81. Cardioid SDC, flat 20 Hz–20 kHz, requires 48V. **The third of three
             SM81s** on this show (hat 5, ride 14, toys 19) — Brian confirmed he owns three.
             SWITCH STATE: **assumed 18 dB/oct low-cut ENGAGED, −10 dB pad OUT**, same reasoning as
             ch 5 and ch 14.

SEARCHES     1. `SM81 aux percussion tray shaker tambourine woodblock mic EQ live high pass 250Hz`
             2. `Shure SM81 hi-hat live sound EQ HPF settings cymbal wash flat response 18dB octave`
                (capsule pass shared across the three SM81 units)

CAPSULE FACT **Flat 20 Hz–20 kHz, no presence peak, no hyped top end**, three-position low-cut
             (flat / −6 dB-oct below 100 Hz / −18 dB-oct below 80 Hz) and a lockable −10 dB pad
             (SoundRef review; Shure product page; Thomann). Directly on point for this source:
             **"percussion instruments with high-end detail — including tambourines, shakers and
             triangles — benefit from the smooth upper frequencies of the SM81"**, and "small
             tambourines, bells and triangles will sound fine with the SM81" (Shure applications
             copy / MicrophoneGeeks SM81 review).

WEB SAYS     The SM81 is a first-call mic for exactly this content because its top end is detailed
             without being harsh — which matters because tambourine and shaker glare in the
             6–10 kHz region is the standard problem with aux percussion in a live mix. A flat
             capsule means that glare is the INSTRUMENT's, not the mic's, and it is legitimate to
             cut it.

KB SAYS      mic-library: "Ruler-flat SDC, clean detailed highs with no harsh fizz… Accurate on
             hat/OH/acoustic — takes EQ well. Weakness: needs care on cymbal wash, no built-in
             flattery." Bias: apply template as-is.

VERDICT      **AGREE.** Web and KB match on the capsule, and the web adds the source-specific
             endorsement (tambourines/shakers/triangles) that the KB's generic "acoustic
             instruments" line implies but doesn't state.

LOCKER       Silent pass. The SM81 is the locker's honest flat SDC and the web names this exact
             content as a strength. Availability resolved in the round.

GENRE BEND   Funk/R&B/Motown: aux percussion is a groove instrument, played continuously, and it
             lives up top where it does not compete with anything else in the arrangement. But it
             is played CLOSE to a condenser at high transient level, so the genre bend is
             restraint: get the sparkle from the flat capsule and cut what glares, rather than
             lifting it. Artist layer: percussion is a texture in this band's sound, not a feature —
             there is no percussion soloist and the congas that were here last week are gone.

VENUE BEND   FSQ outdoor: bleed and box cuts at FSQ depth. HPF runs HIGH at **300** — a toy tray has
             nothing musical below that, and it sits in the middle of a loud stage collecting kick,
             bass and gusts. **Weather layer moved a value off rev 1:** the glare trim at 8 kHz
             LIGHTENS from rev 1's −3 to **−2**, because 38 % RH dry air is already attenuating that
             region over the plaza throw and a full trim on top of the air's own loss leaves the
             percussion dull at the back. LPF opens to 18 kHz for the same reason.

GATE CHECK   **No boosts on this channel — nothing to permit.**
             - **No sparkle boost, and that is a sectional decision as much as a capsule one.** The
               capsule is flat, so a high lift would technically pass the gate — but the hat (ch 5)
               already owns the show's only 10 kHz lift and the ride (ch 14) owns 2500. A third
               flat condenser lifted up top stacks into wash and costs gain-before-feedback with
               four live vocals on an open plaza. The sparkle comes from the capsule and the LPF at
               18 kHz.
             - **B4 −2 @ 8000 is the instrument's glare, not the mic's.** Tambourine and shaker
               transients pile up in the 6–10 kHz band; the SM81 reports that honestly rather than
               creating it, so the cut is corrective. Sized to −2 rather than −3 by the dry-air read.
             - B3 −6 @ 1000 is woodblock/bell-tree honk; B2 −6 @ 400 is stage bleed. Both at FSQ
               depth on a capsule with no voicing to work around.
