CAPSULE FACT (equipment fact) A sampling pad's output is **produced, pre-mixed material at
             line/instrument level** — each sample was EQ'd and compressed before it ever reached
             the pad. There is no capsule and no transducer character to correct. The practical
             consequence: the pad channels are level-and-routing channels with one corrective cut,
             not tone channels.

WEB SAYS     The consistent live-sound position on pads and playback is that the shaping belongs
             upstream in the samples; at FOH you manage level, low-end overlap and headroom. Run as
             two panned mono channels (Brian's rev-1 call), each channel gets identical treatment —
             they are two halves of one instrument's output, not two competing sources.

KB SAYS      `eq-starting-points` has **no sampling-pad row** — one of the four rows in the
             write-back Brian approved today. Nothing in the KB contradicts the web read.

VERDICT      **THIN on the KB side by absence, AGREE on the web side.** Resolved by Brian's own
             rev-1 decision on how the pad is run, plus the staged KB row so the next show inherits
             a baseline.

LOCKER       **Exempt** — DI inputs.

GENRE BEND   The pad shares the horn/string duty with the keys and the Track (no horn channels on
             this list). So like unit 17, the pad's 1–2 kHz window is left alone — cutting there
             would gut the programmed stabs. Artist layer: rehearsed show band, tight arrangements;
             the pad hits are placed and need to land cleanly rather than be shaped.

VENUE BEND   FSQ outdoor: the mud cut at 300 Hz runs to FSQ-appropriate depth (−5), sized down from
             the usual −7/−8 because the samples are already produced and a deep cut would thin
             finished material. HPF 40 keeps the sub content of produced samples — the KS21 arch
             can reproduce it and it is part of the arrangement.

GATE CHECK   **No boosts on either channel — nothing to permit.** Produced samples are the same
             case as the Track (unit 14) and the cab emulation (unit 12): the shaping is already in
             the signal, so a boost stacks on someone else's finished decisions. The one cut at
             300 Hz is room-making against the guitar (−8 @ 300) and the keys (−6 @ 300) rather than
             tone — three sources sharing that octave on a plaza is where a dance mix goes opaque.
             Identical values on 17 and 18 are CORRECT and not a sectional-rule failure: these are
             the left and right of a single instrument's output, panned, and giving them different
             curves would smear the pad's own image.
