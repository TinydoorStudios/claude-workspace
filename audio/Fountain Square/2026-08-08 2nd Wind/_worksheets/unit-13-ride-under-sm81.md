# Unit 13 — Ride cymbal, mic'd FROM BELOW × Shure SM81 · CH 14

INSTRUMENT   Ride cymbal, mic'd from **underneath** in a claw (band's note: "Under mic w claw").
             This is a new channel this week — fader 14 was Edwin's talkback in rev 1. On an
             R&B/funk/Motown set the ride carries the jazz-inflected material and the half-time
             feels; the bell is a hook instrument in gospel-adjacent arrangements. Role in THIS
             mix: bell and stick definition that reads through a dance mix, reinforcing the
             overheads rather than replacing them.

MIC          Shure SM81. Cardioid SDC, flat 20 Hz–20 kHz, requires 48V, claw/rim mount.
             **One of THREE SM81s on this kit** — Brian confirmed he owns three (2026-08-08).
             SWITCH STATE: **assumed 18 dB/oct low-cut ENGAGED, −10 dB pad OUT**, same rationale as
             ch 5 — the KB says use the onboard filter before the desk's, and from underneath this
             mic is aimed up past the floor tom with 15–16 mph gusts on the stage.
             ⚠ **POLARITY INVERT on this channel** — see gate check. This is not optional.

SEARCHES     1. `ride cymbal microphone under the ride from below stick definition bell phase
                overheads live sound`
             2. `Shure SM81 hi-hat live sound EQ HPF settings cymbal wash flat response 18dB octave`
                (capsule pass, shared with unit 05) + Audio-Technica's *2 Drum Mic Tips for

CAPSULE FACT **Flat 20 Hz–20 kHz with no presence peak and no hyped top end**, three-position
             low-cut (**flat / −6 dB per octave below 100 Hz / −18 dB per octave below 80 Hz**) and a
             lockable **−10 dB pad** (SoundRef SM81 review; Shure product page; Thomann specs).
             Reviewers are explicit that "the brightness you get on playback is the brightness of
             the cymbal, not an artifact of the microphone."

WEB SAYS     This is the research that shaped the channel, and it changed three things:
             1. **Under-miking a ride "helps control the ping by providing decent rejection from
                other cymbals and deemphasizing the high pitch pings, while allowing you to add
                body back to the ride in the mix."** So the position itself reduces the top — the
                desk is not fighting an over-bright source, it is restoring definition.
             2. **Position within the underside matters:** "moving the mic closer to the bell will
                add more ping, while moving it to the edge will emphasize the overtones." That is a
                placement note for load-in, not an EQ move.
             3. **Phase is the first thing to deal with:** "when you have mics under the cymbals
                pointing up in the opposite direction of other mics pointing down, the first thing
                to deal with is phase cancellation — reverse the phase on the cymbal mics pointing
                in the opposite direction." And the hierarchy is stated plainly: "with bottom
                micing a ride, the primary mics are the overheads, and the bottom mic is to
                reinforce that sound and add some body back."

KB SAYS      mic-library: "Ruler-flat SDC, clean detailed highs with no harsh fizz, flat/6/18 dB
             HPF. Accurate on hat/OH/acoustic — takes EQ well. Weakness: needs care on cymbal wash,
             no built-in flattery." Bias: apply template as-is (flat/honest). `eq-starting-points`
             has no under-ride row — the position is unusual enough that the KB doesn't cover it,
             which is exactly why the web pass carried this unit.

VERDICT      **AGREE** on the capsule (web and KB both: ruler-flat, no presence peak, switchable
             filter, watch wash). **The technique is web-only** — the KB has nothing on
             under-ride miking — so the placement, the phase call and the "reinforce, don't
             replace" hierarchy all come from external sources with the KB silent rather than
             contradicting. Noted for a possible future KB addition.

LOCKER       Silent pass. mic-library lists the SM81 for "Overheads, hi-hat, **acoustic
             instruments**" and it is the locker's honest flat SDC — the right class of mic for a
             position whose whole point is not to add character. The availability conflict that
             would have forced a fork was resolved in the round (three SM81s).

GENRE BEND   R&B/funk with jazz inflections: the ride's bell is a hook and the stick definition
             carries the half-time and shuffle feels. That earns this channel a real boost — and
             because the under-position *removes* top rather than adding it, and the capsule is
             flat, the boost is honest rather than a stack. Artist layer: they back gospel and R&B
             national acts, where a crisp forward ride bell is idiomatic.

VENUE BEND   FSQ outdoor. HPF runs HIGH at **300** — from underneath this mic is aimed up past the
             floor tom and snare, so bleed from below is its main problem, and gusts make a high
             filter free insurance. Bleed cut at FSQ depth. Weather layer: 38 % RH dry air over the
             plaza throw reinforces the case for the 2500 Hz definition lift — this is the band
             that has to survive the distance for the bell to read at the back.

GATE CHECK   **One boost: B4 +3 @ 2500. The facts that permit it —** two, and they compound:
             (a) the SM81 is documented **flat 20 Hz–20 kHz with no presence peak**, so there is no
             capsule voicing at 2500 to stack on; and (b) the under-ride position itself
             **"deemphasizes the high pitch pings"** per the external placement research, so the
             desk is restoring definition the *placement* removed rather than exaggerating
             something already present. This is a boost with two independent justifications, which
             is the strongest case on this show.
             - **⚠ POLARITY INVERT.** A mic under the cymbals points opposite the overheads, and
               the external guidance is unambiguous: reverse phase on cymbal mics pointing the
               other way. Without it this channel cancels against fader 9 — and since the overheads
               are the PRIMARY cymbal picture here, that cancellation would damage the main source,
               not a garnish. No EQ fixes it.
             - **No high-sizzle boost.** The hat (ch 5) owns the show's only 10 kHz lift. Adding a
               second high boost on another flat condenser in the cymbal group would stack into
               wash and hiss on a plaza. LPF 16 kHz caps it.
             - B3 −6 @ 700 addresses the underside "gong" character and shell bleed; B2 −7 @ 500 is
               floor-tom and snare bleed arriving from below.
             - PLACEMENT NOTE for load-in: closer to the bell = more ping, out toward the edge =
               more overtones. If the bell isn't reading after the +3, move the mic before adding dB.

CYMBAL SLOTTING  (three cymbal mics — the sectional rule in numbers)
             CH 14 Ride       mid/definition **+3 @ 2500** · cuts **700** and **500**
             CH 5  Hat        high **+3 @ 10000** · cut **4000**
             CH 9  Overheads  **−2 @ 9000**, no boost · cut **1200** — the primary picture
             Two boosts across the group, an octave and a half apart, in lanes chosen for what each
             mic and position actually contributes. No shared cut frequency in the mid/high region.
             The hat and overheads do share a 400 Hz bleed cut, which is deliberate rather than an
             oversight — 400 Hz on both is rejection of the same drums, not a lane they compete in,
             and the ride's is offset to 500 because from underneath it sees the floor tom too.
