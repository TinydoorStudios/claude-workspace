# Unit 11 — Electric guitar / Fender Twin Reverb × Electro-Voice N/D 408  (ch 13)

INSTRUMENT   Electric guitar, single guitarist. R&B/neo-soul covers — clean comping, chord
             stabs, the occasional single-note line. Its whole job is to sit under the vocal
             and stay out of its way. Guitars 2–4 are spares and unused.
             **EQUIPMENT: Fender Twin Reverb assumed** (Brian, no rider — stated assumption,
             not a finding).
MIC          **Electro-Voice N/D 408** — swapped in by Brian 2026-07-30 off the sheet's
             Sennheiser e609. First-generation, no letter suffix. Supercardioid N/DYM dynamic on
             a pivoting yoke. Only one in the locker, and it is free this show.
             KB tendency: "ease off presence, attack; tame box ~400Hz."

CAPSULE FACT **30 Hz–22 kHz close / 60 Hz–22 kHz far, 3.1 mV/Pa, 150 Ω, 144 dB dynamic range**;
             brighter and more aggressive through the upper mids than an SM57, with a deliberate
             proximity lift. Source: EV N/D 408B data sheet (Part No. 531818-201, 1992), via the
             KB's own 2026-07-26 research pass, corroborated this session by EV's N/D 408B
             product copy ("percussion, guitar amp and horns"; supercardioid for gain-before-
             feedback).
EQUIPMENT FACT **The Fender blackface Twin Reverb tone stack at typical settings dips from
             ~80 Hz to about 500 Hz and rises from ~1,300 Hz upward** — the classic Fender
             mid-scoop with a forward top. 2× 12" Jensen C-12K, and a bright switch on the
             normal channel. Source: Gearspace "Fender Twin Reverb - Freq response?";
             fenderguru.com BF/SF Twin Reverb.
WEB SAYS     On guitar cab specifically, user reports put the N/D 408 "very similar to the
             Sennheiser e906 (neutral setting)" — flatter and less hyped than a 57 or a 609.
             That is a meaningful difference from the mic it replaced: the e609's own KB row is
             "bright upper-mid/presence ~4-5kHz… can be edgy ~2.5-4kHz on bright amps," and a
             Twin is precisely a bright amp.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "brighter and more aggressive upper mids than an SM57, deliberate
             proximity lift, high SPL, tiny pivoting head that fits where a 421 won't. Weakness:
             that upper-mid bite gets spiky on bright sources; close working distance piles
             low-mids." Tendency: ease off presence, attack; tame box ~400 Hz.
VERDICT      **AGREE** — the KB row and this session's sources both describe an upper-mid-forward
             supercardioid with a proximity lift, and both flag the same failure mode on bright
             sources.
LOCKER       No fork — **Brian made this swap himself** (e609 → N/D 408, 2026-07-30). Recorded
             as a decision, not re-litigated. It is also the better call for the reason above:
             on a Twin, the 609's 4–5 kHz presence lift would have been the third bright thing
             in a row.
GENRE BEND   R&B/neo-soul comping guitar is a texture. It gets carved out of the vocal's
             presence lane deliberately — this is a sectional slotting decision, and the
             vocal wins the 1–3 kHz band outright.
VENUE BEND   FSQ + 96% RH is the decisive layer here, because **three bright things stack**: an
             upper-mid-aggressive capsule, an amp whose tone stack rises from 1.3 kHz up, and
             saturated air that absorbs almost no HF across the throw. Hence the deepest
             high-band cut on the show at −5 @ 4000. HPF 100 for gusts and stage bleed.

GATE CHECK   **No boosts on this channel** — and B1 is the one worth explaining, because the
             equipment layer argued for a boost and the capsule gate overruled it. The Twin's
             tone stack scoops 80–500 Hz, which would normally earn a +3 @ 200 to put the body
             back. It does not get one: at the assumed working distance (2–3" off the grille)
             the N/D 408's **deliberate proximity lift already piles low-mids in exactly that
             region**, so a desk boost would stack on it. The body comes from mic placement,
             and if the guitar reads thin at soundcheck the fix is to move the mic, not the EQ.
             B3 −5 @ 1600 is sectional slotting made visible in the numbers: **the guitar is cut
             where the vocals are not** — ch 33/34/35 are cut at 4000/3500/4500, and this
             channel takes its carve a full octave lower so the two are not fighting for the
             same band.
