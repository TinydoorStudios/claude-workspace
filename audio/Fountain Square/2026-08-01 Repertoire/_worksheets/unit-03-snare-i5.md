# Unit 03 — Snare Top × Audix i5  (ch 3)

INSTRUMENT   Snare, backbeat of an R&B/neo-soul covers set. Sizes and head unknown (no rider).
             Role: the pocket. Neo-soul wants it fat and round, not cracking.
MIC          Audix i5. Cardioid dynamic, VLM diaphragm, no switches.
             KB tendency: "ease off body, crack, snap; tame harsh ~5500Hz."
SEARCHES     "Audix i5 snare frequency response presence peak 5kHz dB live EQ R&B"
             → recordinghacks.com/microphones/Audix/i5, soundonsound.com/reviews/audix-i5,
               audixusa.com/products/i5, rhythmnotes.net SM57-vs-i5 comparison
CAPSULE FACT **+9 dB peak at 5500 Hz**, plus a lift around 150 Hz and a broad presence region
             3–8 kHz. Range 50 Hz–16 kHz. Mids slightly scooped versus an SM57.
             Source: RecordingHacks Audix i5 / Sound On Sound i5 review.
WEB SAYS     The 5 kHz bump is why it is recommended over a 57 on snare — "you would not have
             to EQ these recordings as you would using the Shure." Open top end that is not
             brittle. The same peak is the failure mode on an already-cracky snare.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "Body lift ~+5dB@150Hz, presence ~+9dB@5.5kHz, mids slightly scooped
             vs SM57 — more open, more body. Weakness: that 5.5k peak can get harsh on a cracky
             snare." Same numbers.
VERDICT      **AGREE** — +9 dB @ 5.5 kHz and the 150 Hz body lift are stated identically on both
             sides.
LOCKER       **FORK RAISED** — Audix i5 (DP8) vs Lauten LS-408 (standalone). See fork card in
             the question round. Recommendation: keep the i5.
GENRE BEND   R&B/neo-soul snare sits fat and controlled. Community guidance for the genre is
             explicit that low-mid warmth is a feature, not mud — so the reflex 250–400 Hz gut
             does not happen here; the cut moves up to 500 Hz where the problem is honk rather
             than warmth.
VENUE BEND   FSQ: HPF 180 (gusts to 28 mph, kick bleed, no room gain). **96% RH is the deciding
             layer on the top** — saturated air absorbs almost no HF, so the +9 dB @ 5.5 kHz
             arrives at the back of the plaza intact and gets trimmed harder than it would on a
             dry night.
DRAFT BANDS  HPF 180 · LPF 16000
             B4  −4 | 5500 | 2   | BELL
             B3  −6 | 500  | 2   | BELL
             B2  −3 | 1000 | 2   | BELL
             B1  FLAT
GATE CHECK   **No boosts on this channel.** The two things a snare EQ normally reaches for are
             both already in the capsule: crack (+9 dB @ 5.5 kHz) and body (+5 dB @ 150 Hz).
             So B4 trims the peak instead of adding to it, and B1 stays FLAT instead of adding
             body the capsule already supplies. B2 −3 @ 1000 takes the papery upper-mid ping off
             so the drum reads round — that region is the i5's slight scoop, so the cut is light.
QUESTIONS    Locker fork (see round). Snare wires/tuning unknown — the 500 Hz depth is the value
             most likely to move at soundcheck.
