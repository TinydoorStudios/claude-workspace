# Unit 06 — Rack toms × Audix D2  (ch 6 Rack 1, ch 7 Rack 2)

INSTRUMENT   Two rack toms, R&B/neo-soul covers. Sizes unknown (no rider). Role: fills and
             turnarounds, not a rock tom sound — warm and quick, gone as soon as they land.
MIC          Audix D2 ×2 (DP8). Hypercardioid dynamic, VLM diaphragm, no switches.
             KB tendency: "ease off boom, mud."
SEARCHES     "Audix D2 D4 tom mic frequency response hypercardioid peak dB live EQ specification"
             → recordinghacks.com/microphones/Audix/D2, audixusa.com/products (D2),
               thomannmusic.com/audix_d2_drum_microphone.htm,
               homerecording.com "Differnece between Audix D2 and D4"
CAPSULE FACT **Boost around 150 Hz, a dip between 500 Hz and 1 kHz, and a subtle upper-mid
             presence lift.** Range 68 Hz–18 kHz. Larger low-frequency boost than the D4.
             Source: RecordingHacks Audix D2 (reading Audix's published chart).
WEB SAYS     Developed specifically with rack toms in mind; "full, weighty" with a scooped-mid
             curve. The 150 Hz boost is placed where rack-tom body sits. Community reports it
             needs less low-end help than a general-purpose dynamic.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "Rack-tom hypercardioid: +150Hz body, dip 500Hz-1kHz, subtle upper-mid
             lift. Punchy, articulate. Weakness: leaner deep lows than D4." Same three features,
             same frequencies.
VERDICT      **AGREE** — 150 Hz boost, 500 Hz–1 kHz dip and the upper-mid lift are stated
             identically on both sides.
LOCKER       Silent pass. mic-library's tom row for the D2 is "Toms — small/mid toms," which is
             exactly this. The MD 421-U and the N/D 408 are the locker's other tom answers, but
             the 421 is a first-choice *floor/large* tom mic here and the single N/D 408 is
             committed to ch 13 (guitar) this show, so neither is free as a matched rack pair.
GENRE BEND   R&B fills want warmth and speed, not attack. Nothing on either channel reaches for
             stick click.
VENUE BEND   FSQ: HPF 100 / 90 (gusts, kick and snare bleed, no room gain). Box cuts run at FSQ
             depth (−6). 96% RH means the D2's upper-mid lift arrives intact, so both channels
             trim it rather than leaving it.
             **Sectional slotting — the two toms own different lanes and no value is shared:**
             Rack 1 (higher) sits above Rack 2 in every band.
DRAFT BANDS  **Ch 6 — Rack 1**   HPF 100 · LPF 12000
             B4  −3 | 6000 | 2   | BELL
             B3  −4 | 700  | 2   | BELL
             B2  −6 | 350  | 2   | BELL
             B1  FLAT

             **Ch 7 — Rack 2**   HPF 90 · LPF 12000
             B4  −3 | 5500 | 2   | BELL
             B3  −4 | 600  | 2   | BELL
             B2  −6 | 320  | 2   | BELL
             B1  FLAT
GATE CHECK   **No boosts on either channel.** B1 is FLAT on both specifically because the
             capsule bakes in +150 Hz of tom body — a low boost here would stack directly on
             that lift, which is the failure this gate exists to catch. The body comes from the
             mic, not the desk.
             Capsule gate in reverse on B3: the D2 already dips 500 Hz–1 kHz, so the desk cut
             sits at −4 rather than the FSQ reflex −6 to −8. Cutting hard into a baked scoop
             would hollow the toms out.
             Sectional check: Rack 1 and Rack 2 share no frequency and no depth — 6000/5500,
             700/600, 350/320, HPF 100/90.
QUESTIONS    Tom sizes unknown. If the rack toms turn out to be unusually large (13"/14"), the
             two HPFs and the 350/320 pair both come down a step.
