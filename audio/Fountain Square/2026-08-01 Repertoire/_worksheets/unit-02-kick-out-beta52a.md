             KB tendency: "ease off attack, box, mud."
SEARCHES     "Shure Beta 52A kick drum frequency response boost 4kHz scoop midrange dB specification"
             → shure.com Beta 52A, drumhelper.com review, Shure Beta52a spec sheet (content-files.shure.com)
             "Beta 52A frequency response chart dip 300Hz 400Hz low mid gearspace kick two mic blend 91A 52A"

CAPSULE FACT **Published response 20 Hz–10 kHz; a big dip around 400 Hz and a peak centred at
             4 kHz for beater click** — the mic is effectively pre-EQing the kick the way most
             engineers would. Max SPL 174 dB.
             Source: Shure Beta 52A spec sheet (20–10,000 Hz) + Gearspace "Shure Beta 52 vs
             Audix D-6 (Kick Drum)" thread.
WEB SAYS     Consensus calls it "decidedly modern because of the mid-cut and the boom/beater
             emphasis." The 4–4.5 kHz lift is specifically the beater striking the head. Rock
             and metal guidance recommends a further 300 Hz cut on top of the baked dip.
KB SAYS      mic-library: "Tailored kick dynamic, scooped low-mids, presence lift ~4kHz, big
             lows. Click+thump voiced in." Same shape, same frequencies.
VERDICT      **AGREE** — web and KB name the same 4 kHz lift and the same low-mid scoop.
LOCKER       First-call match — mic-library's standard two-mic kick rig is exactly Beta 52 +
             Beta 91. No fork.
GENRE BEND   R&B/neo-soul: the kick's job is weight and roundness. Its baked 4 kHz click is
             redundant here because unit 01 already owns the attack, so it comes down rather
             than being left to double the click.
VENUE BEND   FSQ has zero room gain, so nothing reinforces the bottom for free — this is the
             one channel that earns a low boost, and it stays modest (+3, not +6) because eight
             KS21s per side in a delayed arch already own everything below 45 Hz. HPF 45.

GATE CHECK   One boost: **B1 +3 @ 55.** Permitted because the 52A's low end is a *broad
             extension* down to 20 Hz, not a narrow baked peak — the capsule's baked features
             are the 400 Hz dip and the 4 kHz lift, neither of which is anywhere near 55 Hz.
             Capsule gate in reverse on B3: the capsule already dips at ~400, so the desk cut
             sits **beside** it at 550 and runs −5 rather than the FSQ reflex −8. Cutting on top
             of a baked scoop would hollow the kick out.
             Two-mic pair with unit 01: **this mic owns BODY/SUB, top and bottom.** Its baked
             4 kHz beater peak is trimmed −5 precisely so it does not stack with unit 01's
             4–9 kHz attack emphasis, and unit 01's B1 is FLAT so the bottom is not stacked
             either. LPF 7000 keeps it out of the attack lane entirely.
