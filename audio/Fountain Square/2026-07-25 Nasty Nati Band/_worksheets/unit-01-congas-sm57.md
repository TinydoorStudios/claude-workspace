# Unit 01 — Congas / bongos × Shure SM57  (ch 15, 16)

INSTRUMENT   Hand drums, played standing at the front of a second-line brass band. Confirmed
             congas/bongos by Brian this rev (Rev 1.0 had it flagged "not confirmed"). Short
             stands on the sheet corroborate low hand drums, not mounted small perc. Role: the
             second-line groove engine under the horn block — has to read as attack + body,
             not as another low-end source competing with sousaphone and bass.
MIC          Shure SM57, no phantom, Short stand. KB mic-library: "Snare (primary), guitar cab
             (primary), utility anywhere." Not switchable. Not a ribbon. Not TOUR. Single mic
             per drum, no blend pair.
SEARCHES     1. "Shure SM57 frequency response presence peak dB 5kHz proximity low end rolloff spec"
             2. "congas live sound EQ frequency fundamental Hz slap tone mic SM57 high pass filter"
CAPSULE FACT SM57 presence rises from 2 kHz and PEAKS AT +7 dB @ 6 kHz; low end rolls off from
             ~200 Hz with a minor baked dip 250–600 Hz; range 40 Hz–15 kHz.
             Source: RecordingHacks / Shure SM57 published response + soundref review.
WEB SAYS     Congas: fundamental ~200 Hz, harmonics at 400 Hz and 700 Hz, open-slap fundamental
             ~1 kHz. HPF ~100 Hz recommended for modern/live work. A narrow cut ~250 Hz reduces
             feedback when miking congas. Generic advice says boost ~5 kHz for "crispy slaps."
             SM57 specifically called out as a long-standing live conga mic.
             Sources: audiorecording.me conga mixing guide, Sound on Sound "Recording Latin
             Percussion", congaplace + Gearspace threads.
KB SAYS      mic-library: SM57 = mid-forward workhorse, presence 3–5k, builds box 300–500.
             eq-starting-points: percussion conservative unless the genre says otherwise.
VERDICT      AGREE — web and KB both put the 57's character in the same place (a real presence
             peak in the 5–6k region and a low-mid box tendency). The web adds the quantitative
             +7 dB @ 6 kHz figure the KB lacks. No fork, nothing to ask.
LOCKER       First-call match. mic-library lists the SM57 as the utility percussion answer and
             the fresh pass independently names it a standard live conga mic. No alternative
             offered — an i5 or D2 would be a taste swap, not a nameable win.
GENRE BEND   Second-line/funk: the congas are groove, not colour. They need slap definition to
             cut a seven-horn block, and they must NOT add weight below 100 — the sousaphone
             (+3@90) and bass (HPF 40) already own that. So: no low boost, and the box cut runs
             at FSQ depth rather than a polite indoor trim.
VENUE BEND   FSQ outdoor, no room gain: cuts run deeper and the HPF is set decisively. Rev 1.0's
             blanket HPF 120 is wrong for a confirmed conga — it clips the 200 Hz fundamental's
             lower skirt. Split the pair by pitch instead.
DRAFT BANDS  ch 15 "Perc 1" (lower drum / conga)   HPF 90  | LPF OFF
               B4 −2 @ 6000 Q1.5 BELL · B3 −3 @ 700 Q2 BELL · B2 −4 @ 250 Q2 BELL · B1 FLAT
             ch 16 "Perc 2" (higher drum / bongo)  HPF 140 | LPF OFF
               B4 −2 @ 6500 Q1.5 BELL · B3 −3 @ 800 Q2 BELL · B2 −4 @ 300 Q2 BELL · B1 FLAT
GATE CHECK   NO BOOSTS ON THIS UNIT — and that is the gate doing its job. The web's standard
             advice is "boost ~5 kHz for crispy slaps." The SM57 already bakes in +7 dB @ 6 kHz,
             so that boost would stack straight onto a voiced peak. The gate's correct move is a
             trim or nothing: taking −2 @ 6k still leaves roughly +5 dB of the mic's own peak
             doing the slap definition, minus the outdoor harshness through the A15s.
             Reverse gate: the 57 has a MINOR baked dip 250–600 Hz, so the box cut is held to
             −4 rather than the −6..−9 FSQ default — deep-cutting an already-scooped zone is the
             same error in the other direction.
