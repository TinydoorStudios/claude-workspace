# Unit 02 — Baritone sax × Audio-Technica PRO 35  (ch 21)

INSTRUMENT   Baritone saxophone, clip-on at the bell, in a seven-piece horn line. Confirmed by
             Brian this rev — the sheet only said "Sax 3". Low Bb/A puts the fundamental around
             65–69 Hz, but in a second-line band the bari plays the low horn LINE, not the bass:
             the sousaphone is the bass. So the bari's job is midrange weight and grit under the
             tenor, not bottom octave.
MIC          Audio-Technica PRO 35, phantom required, Clip mount. SWITCHABLE HARDWARE — the body
             carries an −18 dB @ 80 Hz high-pass SWITCH. Assumed position: OUT (flat), confirmed
             by Brian this rev for both low horns. Not a ribbon. Not TOUR. Single mic.
SEARCHES     1. "Audio-Technica PRO 35 clip-on baritone sax live sound EQ frequency response"
             2. "baritone sax live mic EQ honk frequency cut brass band horn section"
             3. fetched recordinghacks.com PRO-35 spec page for published figures
CAPSULE FACT PRO 35: published response 50–15,000 Hz, max SPL 145 dB, and "Filter: HPF −18 dB
             @ 80 Hz (Via Switch)" — a SWITCHED filter, not a fixed roll-off.
             Source: RecordingHacks Audio-Technica PRO-35 spec page.
WEB SAYS     Bari sax: mud/honk concentrated 240–400 Hz, and bari carries MORE low mud than the
             smaller saxes. Honk/nasal band 500 Hz–1.6 kHz; presence/edge 2–6 kHz; definition
             6–8 kHz. A working live engineer's rule from Sax on the Web: cut around 2.5 kHz,
             sometimes steeply, and for tenor the bell resonance sits ~630–700 Hz — which scales
             DOWN for the larger bari horn. PRO 35 user reports describe it as slightly
             treble-forward, "nothing a little EQ can't fix."
             Sources: RecordingHacks + Audio-Technica specs, Sax on the Web live-EQ thread,
             musicproductionnerds / musicalinstrumenthub sax EQ guides.
KB SAYS      mic-library PRO 35: "Clip-on cardioid condenser, 50Hz-15kHz (limited top), VOICED
             PRESENCE LIFT, clamp mount. Drums/brass. Weakness: rolled-off extreme top, thin
             lows, plasticky upper-mid if close." Correction column: "ease off presence."
VERDICT      AGREE — the web's "treble-oriented" user reports and the KB's "voiced presence lift
             / ease off presence" are the same finding from two directions, and both flag the
             rolled extreme top. The web contributes the switch fact and the 145 dB SPL figure
             the KB doesn't carry. The one genuine correction is to REV 1.0, not to the KB: Rev
             1.0 wrote the 80 Hz roll-off as inherent. It isn't. Flagged to Brian, answered
             (switch OUT), and queued as a KB write-back.
LOCKER       First-call match — the PRO 35 is the clip-on Brian is already running across the
             whole horn line, and section consistency is itself the win here. Suggesting a 4099
             for one horn out of seven would split the section's voicing for no nameable gain.
GENRE BEND   Second-line brass: the bari is a rhythmic honk instrument, played hard, doubling
             riffs under the tenor. It needs its own lane in a seven-horn block or it disappears
             into the sousaphone. Lane assigned at 600 Hz — below the tenor's 800, above the
             sousa's 350 mud cut.
VENUE BEND   FSQ outdoor: HPF set high and decisively at 70 to keep the bari OUT of the
             sousaphone (+3@90) and bass (HPF 40) stack — this is the venue's biggest single
             bend on this channel. Mud cut runs a step deeper than its section neighbours
             (−5 vs −3/−4) because the research specifically says bari carries more low mud.
             LPF 10k matching the trombone: no useful content above it and the mic is rolled
             there anyway.

GATE CHECK   NO BOOST ON THIS CHANNEL, deliberately. The generic sax advice is to lift 6–8 kHz
             for definition, and every other horn in Rev 1.0 got a top boost (bone +2@6k, tenor
             +3@7k, alto +3@8k, tpt +2@9k). The bari does not get one: the KB's explicit
             correction for this mic is "ease off presence," the mic is voiced with a presence
             lift, and the bari is the DARK horn of the section — its identity is weight, not
             air. Boosting the top here would both stack the capsule's lift and erase the
             contrast that makes the section read as a section.
             The other horns' 7–9 kHz boosts still pass the gate, because those sit ABOVE the
             presence lift in the region the KB calls "rolled-off extreme top" — compensating a
             roll-off, not stacking a peak.
QUESTIONS    None outstanding — both this unit's forks (which horn, switch position) went into
             the question round and came back answered.
