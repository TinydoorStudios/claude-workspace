# Unit 05 — Overheads × Earthworks SR20sp Gen 2 (ch 7, 8 — stereo pair)

**INSTRUMENT** — The whole Gretsch maple kit, from above. **In THIS show this is not a cymbal
channel — it is the most important pair on the drum riser.** See GENRE BEND: the reference record's
kit was one microphone in a room, and this pair is how that gets rebuilt.

**MIC** — Earthworks SR20sp Gen 2, matched stereo pair, from the DK-6. Cardioid. No switches. Not
a ribbon, not TOUR. Stereo-linked.

**SEARCHES**
1. `Earthworks SR25 SR20sp Gen 2 DM17 frequency response flat review drum overheads hi-hat live`
2. Direct fetch: earthworksaudio.com/drum-microphones/sr20sp-g2/ (manufacturer spec + copy)

**CAPSULE FACT** — **20 Hz – 20 kHz**, cardioid, **148 dB SPL @ 3% THD**, sensitivity
**−50 dBV/Pa (3.2 mV/Pa)**, self-noise **25 dBA** — Earthworks SR20sp Gen 2 product spec. The
148 dB ceiling is the operative number: this pair can sit close over a hard-hit kit without ever
being the thing that breaks up.

**WEB SAYS** — Earthworks positions it as their *"most mix-ready instrument microphone,"* with a
*"tight cardioid pattern"* giving *"focus, detail and musicality at the front with astounding
rejection from the back, across all frequencies."* And the line that reorganized this whole drum
section — an Earthworks-featured engineer, verbatim:

> **"With a kick drum and these two overhead mics, I don't need to add any other mic inputs into
> the drum kit."**

**KB SAYS** — `mic-library.md`: *"Earthworks SR20sp Gen 2 — Flat, fast SDC, extended top, very
accurate OH/room. Weakness: reveals harshness honestly, no hype."* EQ tendency: **"apply template
as-is (flat/honest)."**

**VERDICT — AGREE.**
Flat, accurate, no hype, high SPL — maker and KB in lockstep, no contradiction to resolve.

**LOCKER** — **First-call match, no alternative.** The locker's other matched OH pairs are the sE8
(V Pack Arena) and the Audix M1280B (DP8). Neither has a nameable win: both are lower-SPL and
neither is more accurate, and either would break the all-DK-6 kit. Specified pair stands.

**GENRE BEND — this is the structural decision of the show, so it goes on the record here.**

The Back to Black sessions, per mix engineer **Tom Elmhirst** (MusicRadar): *"The drums were
recorded with **one microphone**, and there's lots of spill between the instruments, which was
great."* Drums, piano, guitar and bass tracked together in one room, to 1" 16-track tape.

We are pointing **eight** flat, fast, honest condensers at a kit and asked to reproduce a record
made with **one**. The generic "dense soul/R&B" modifier in the KB pushes the wrong way here —
*more aggressive EQ for separation, faster comp, tighter gates* — and the artist profile outranks
it. **So: this pair is the "one microphone." The close mics (1–6) are fill, not the source.**

Earthworks' own featured engineer says the quiet part out loud — kick plus these two and you need
nothing else. That is not marketing I'm taking on faith; it is a working engineer describing
exactly the architecture this record needs, using exactly this pair.

**Everything downstream follows from that:**
- The HPF stays **low and gentle** — this pair needs shell body, not a cymbal-only tail. A modern
  200 Hz OH HPF would throw away the glue and is banned on this channel.
- **No gate. No ducker.** The wash and the spill *are* the record. Any dynamics here fight the
  brief.
- The close mics get their **partial, range-limited** gates precisely because this pair is holding
  the kit together underneath them.
- Reverb send: this pair is the natural home for the plate.

**VENUE BEND** — Memo cuts both ways here. The 1.6 s RT60 does the Spector work for free, which is
good — but the room's **200** and **250–315** nodes will pile into an open, low-HPF'd overhead
pair harder than into any close mic. Those get treated. The **63 Hz** node is handled by the HPF
rather than a band. Indoor depth. Polarity must be checked against kick and snare before this pair
advances — with the HPF this low, a polarity error will gut the kit's low-mids and read as "the
overheads sound thin," which is the trap.

**DRAFT BANDS** (Q225 layout, whole dB, cuts first) — **linked stereo pair, identical both sides**

| Band | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **HPF** | **100 Hz, 12 dB/oct** | **Deliberately low and deliberately gentle.** This pair carries shell body, not just cymbals. The soft 12 dB/oct slope preserves the glue while the corner drops kick rumble and Memo's 63 Hz node. |
| **LPF** | **14 kHz, 12 dB/oct** | The tape ceiling, same as the hat. The SR20sp runs flat to 20 kHz; *Back to Black* does not. |
| **Band 4 (HF)** | **−3 dB @ 8 kHz, Q 1.5, Bell** | Cymbal sizzle. The KB warns this pair *"reveals harshness honestly"* — in a 556-seat room through a tuned PA, honest reads bright. Lands it in 1966. |
| **Band 3** | **−4 dB @ 315 Hz, Q 1.5, Bell** | Memo's 250–315 node plus the low-mid wash a 1.6 s room builds into an open pair. Deepest cut here — this is the channel most exposed to the room. |
| **Band 2** | **−3 dB @ 200 Hz, Q 2.0, Bell** | Memo's 200 Hz node. |
| **Band 1 (LF)** | **OFF** | The HPF owns everything below 100. |

**Zero boosts.**

**GATE CHECK** — No boosts to justify. Reverse gate: at 20 Hz–20 kHz flat with no published
contour, there is nothing baked to correct — the 8 kHz cut and the LPF are the tape/artist
reference, and 315/200 are the room.

**DYNAMICS**
- **No gate. No ducker.** Stated as a positive decision, not an omission. **What must survive: the
  spill.** Elmhirst called the bleed *"great"*; it is the mechanism that makes eight mics sound
  like one. Gating this pair would be the single most damaging move available on this show.
- **Comp:** Mustard **Blue (Neve)** — glue, not control. **3:1, attack 30 ms** (every transient
  passes), release 200 ms, **2–3 dB GR only.** The record went through a Neve to tape; this is the
  channel that should sound like it.

**QUESTIONS** — none. Mic resolved, verdict AGREE, no locker fork, no switches. The one action item
is procedural, not a question: **polarity-check ch 7/8 against kick and snare at soundcheck before
advancing the pair** — mandatory given the 100 Hz HPF.
