# Unit 03 — Hi-Hat × Earthworks SR25 (ch 3)

**INSTRUMENT** — Hi-hat on a Yamaha HS850 3-leg stand. In THIS band it is load-bearing: the
straight-8ths/shuffle hat pattern is the engine under *Rehab* and *Valerie*, and on the *Frank*-side
material it drops to brushes and quiet ride/hat work. Needs to be crisp but never spitty.

**MIC** — Earthworks SR25, from the DK-25 (×3 in the kit). **Cardioid** — confirmed against the
manufacturer data sheet, matching the KB and Brian's inventory. (Note: the current *Gen 2* SR25 is
sold as supercardioid; Brian's DK-25 pair is the cardioid version. No conflict — the KB is right.)
No switches. Not a ribbon, not TOUR.

**SEARCHES**
1. `Earthworks SR25 SR20sp Gen 2 DM17 frequency response flat review drum overheads hi-hat live`
2. Direct fetch: **Earthworks SR25 Data Sheet V1** (manufacturer PDF, Specifications table)

**CAPSULE FACT** — **20 Hz to 25 kHz, +2 dB @ 30 cm** — Earthworks SR25 Data Sheet, Specifications
table, verbatim. Also: Cardioid · **145 dB SPL** max acoustic input · sensitivity **10 mV/Pa
(−40 dBV/Pa)** · self-noise **20 dBA** · *"Uniform Frequency Response at 0° 45° & 90° off-axis."*

**That +2 dB tolerance across five octaves is the whole story: this mic bakes in nothing.**

**WEB SAYS** — Consensus and maker agree it is essentially ruler-flat: *"the frequency response is
flat, apart from a gentle reduction in the low frequencies below 100 Hz and a little rise above
10 kHz"* — and the data sheet bounds even that inside +2 dB. Earthworks' pitch is *"more gain
before feedback"* and a *"near-perfect cardioid polar pattern … consistent across all frequencies."*

**KB SAYS** — `mic-library.md`: *"Earthworks SR25 — Flat cardioid SDC, fast transients, natural top
end for hat/OH. Weakness: uncolored — flatters nothing."* EQ tendency: **"apply template as-is
(flat/honest)."**

**VERDICT — AGREE.**
Manufacturer says ±2 dB flat, cardioid. KB says flat cardioid, uncolored, apply the template
as-is. Same claim, same pattern, and the maker's own number bounds it. Solid sourced baseline —
and it means every move below is a real tonal decision I own, not a correction of the mic.

**LOCKER** — **First-call match, no alternative.** The KB lists the SR25 for *"Overheads (×2) or
kick (×1)"* and the locker's other hat options are the Shure SM81 and the Audix M1280BHC. Neither
offers a nameable win here: the SM81 is equally flat with a lower SPL ceiling, and the M1280BHC is
a clip mic whose value is compactness, not sound. The SR25 also keeps the kit inside the Earthworks
family. Specified mic stands.

**GENRE BEND** — This is where the artist profile does all the work, because the mic contributes
nothing. *Back to Black* was cut to **1" 16-track tape through a Neve** — tape rolls the top off.
A ruler-flat condenser on a hat, into a tuned PA in a 556-seat room, will read markedly brighter
and spittier than the reference. **The darkening is therefore mine to add, deliberately, at the
desk** — it is not correction, it is the record.

**VENUE BEND** — Memo. The KB rule for hat is *"HPF aggressively"* — and here the HPF earns
double: set at 300 Hz it clears the room's **250–315 Hz node** off this channel entirely, so no
bell band is spent on it. Kick/snare spill below that goes with it. Indoor depth.

**DRAFT BANDS** (Q225 layout, whole dB, cuts first)

| Band | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **HPF** | **300 Hz, 18 dB/oct** | KB's "HPF aggressively" — nothing musical on a hat below it. Doubles as the fix for Memo's 250–315 node, and kills kick/snare spill. |
| **LPF** | **14 kHz, 12 dB/oct** | The tape ceiling. The SR25 runs flat to 25 kHz; the reference record does not. |
| **Band 4 (HF)** | **−3 dB @ 8 kHz, Q 1.5, Bell** | Sizzle. Takes the modern hi-fi shimmer off the 8ths and lands it in 1966. |
| **Band 3** | **−3 dB @ 2.5 kHz, Q 1.5, Bell** | The spit in the stick attack. Keeps the pattern crisp without the glassy edge a flat condenser gives it through a big PA. |
| **Band 2** | **−3 dB @ 400 Hz, Q 1.5, Bell** | Snare-bleed body sitting just above the HPF's corner, where an 18 dB/oct slope hasn't fully taken hold. |
| **Band 1 (LF)** | **OFF** | The 300 Hz HPF owns everything below. Nothing left to do. |

**Zero boosts.**

**GATE CHECK** — No boosts to justify. Reverse gate: nothing on this channel is a correction of a
baked capsule feature, because at **+2 dB across 20 Hz–25 kHz there are no baked features.** Every
cut is traceable to the room (400) or the tape/artist reference (2.5k, 8k, LPF).

**DYNAMICS**
- **Ducker — NOT a gate.** Threshold **−50 dB, range 30 dB**, straight off the KB's
  acoustic-forward spec. Two reasons converge: the KB's own rule (*"prefer a light ducker over a
  hard gate on acoustic-forward shows — it pulls the hat back during silences without hard-gating
  the cymbal tail"*), and this show's one-mic reference, where chopping the kit into isolated
  channels is exactly the wrong sound. **What must survive: brushes, and the hat's tail.**
- **Comp:** none. The hat does not need it and compression would pump the bleed.

**QUESTIONS** — none. Mic resolved, pattern confirmed against the maker, verdict AGREE, no locker
fork, no switches.
