Band Context
The Brit Pack — British-born session musicians performing a definitive survey of UK rock and pop, 60s through 90s: Beatles, Stones, Led Zeppelin, Queen, The Who, Kinks, Cream, David Bowie, Elton John, The Police, Oasis, Radiohead and more. Personnel: Matt Nakoa (lead vox / guitar / keys), Mark Johnson (guitar / vox), Bryan Percivall (bass / vox), Will Haywood Smith (drums / vox).
Sonic target: Warm, punchy, period-appropriate. Classic British rock tones — not modern hyper-compressed stadium sound. Think Bonham's room, Page's Les Paul through a Supro, Watts's loose snare, McCartney's Hofner. Let the dynamics breathe. The MD421s on guitar cabs will deliver exactly the midrange bite these tones need.

SHOW NOTES & WATCHPOINTS
⚠ PHANTOM POWER
Only Ch 1 (Beta 91A boundary mic) requires 48V phantom. No other channel on this show needs phantom. All DIs are passive Radial ProDIs. Handheld vocals (Beta 58A, sE V7) are dynamics — no phantom. Verify before enabling phantom on any channel other than Ch 1.
GUITAR LAYER — Ch 13 + Ch 14
Guitar Center has both a MD421 (Ch 13, amp) and a Radial ProDI (Ch 14) on the same guitarist position. The DI is a parallel clean signal — confirm intended use with BIS at load-in (tone blend vs. backup). Both channels have independent EQ starting points in the channel processing section below. Group to the same VCA (VCA 4) and check for 300–500 Hz buildup on the guitar bus after both are in the mix.
DUAL VOCAL — Ch 17 + Ch 18 (Matt)
Two sE V7 mics assigned to Matt. These likely cover two different physical positions (center stage + keys/piano position) rather than simultaneous use. Confirm routing intent at production meeting. Treat as two independent vocal channels — do not sum to mono without a polarity check first. Both have identical EQ starting points in the channel processing section below.
OVERHEADS — SM57 x2 (Ch 7 + Ch 8)
Band is traveling with SM57s on overheads. Unconventional but will work — the 57's presence peak delivers a forward, defined cymbal picture suited to classic rock. Top-end air is limited compared to SDCs; EQ starting points in the channel processing section compensate with a +2 dB shelf at 10 kHz. Link Ch 7 and Ch 8 as a stereo pair on the Q225. Polarity check both OHs against close drum mics in mono before soundcheck.
IEM FEED — Monitor Send 6
IEM Output 1 (Monitor Send 6) — confirm recipient at load-in. Likely Matt or Bryan based on vocal intensity of front-of-stage position. Confirm IEM make/model and whether they are using their own pack or house pack.
MONITOR CENTER — Send 3 DUAL WEDGE
Send 3 is a dual-wedge position at center stage shared between Matt and Bryan (lead vocal position). This is the highest-use monitor position on the stage. Budget extra gain-before-feedback headroom on Ch 17 (Matt) and Ch 16 (Bryan) — both will be open simultaneously at this position during shared-mic moments.
SPLIT PATCH TOPOLOGY
Stage Box B drops B3 and B4 (Matt's sE V7 mics) originate from the keys riser position — separate from the main drum/backline Box A. Confirm split chain continuity and that Box B feeds correctly to Ch 17 and Ch 18 at patch. The "B" numbering in the drop column distinguishes these from Box A's numeric drops.

Critical Notes — Reverberant Room (RT60 ≈ 1.6s) + Dense British Rock Show

  • All reverb mix % assume 100% wet (send/return). Adjust channel sends as needed.
  • Use LESS reverb than feels right in isolation — the Memo's 1.6s room adds reverb to everything. Dense rock shows in reverberant rooms go muddy fast. Start conservative.
  • Vocal EQ is CUTS ONLY on every channel. No exceptions. This is feedback control at Memo, not a stylistic choice.
  • Memo problem frequencies (63 Hz, 125 Hz, 200 Hz, 250–315 Hz) accumulate on every open mic — all channels have been treated, but ear-check against the room during soundcheck.
  • Three guitar channels (Ch 13, 14, 15) plus bass (Ch 9, 10) are all competing in the 200–400 Hz range. The cuts at 200 Hz and 400 Hz on guitar channels, and DEQ at 250 Hz on bass, are the separation moves that keep the low-mid from turning to mud in a loud show.
  • Five vocal channels (Ch 16–20) open simultaneously is a significant feedback risk. Set gain structure first, gates before EQ, DEQ last. Tighten gates before adding reverb sends.
  • Bass blend (Ch 9 DI + Ch 10 amp): set Ch 9 for fundamental and definition, bring Ch 10 in from zero until you hear the amp's tone character without the mud. Blend is typically Ch 10 sitting 4–8 dB below Ch 9.
  • Guitar bus: check 300–500 Hz buildup after Ch 13 and Ch 14 are both in the mix. Notch -2 to -3 dB on the guitar bus EQ if needed.
  • Dynamic EQ settings are calibrated for a live reverberant environment. Trust the room.

Workflow Reminders

  • Set gains FIRST — target RMS -25 to -20 dBFS at headamp before applying any EQ or dynamics.
  • Gate thresholds are calibrated for that gain structure. Verify at soundcheck before tweaking.
  • POLARITY CHECK — Overheads (Ch 7/8): check against close drum mics in mono BEFORE soundcheck. Verify as a linked pair, not independently.
  • Link stereo pairs: Overheads (Ch 7/8) • Keys (Ch 11/12).
  • All 20 channels are within patcher range. Run apply_britpack.py before show — copy-and-patch from brian memo v2.ses only. Never edit the template directly.
  • VCA groups: Kit (Ch 1–8) • Backline (Ch 9–15) • Vocals (Ch 16–20).
  • Crowd mic rig (OM1 flown / Deity S2 under PA / CM4 balcony) — patch per standard Memo rig every show. EQ locked per venue-notes.md; do not change.
  • Check bass blend (Ch 9 + Ch 10) in mono before opening faders to full. Phase cancellation between DI and amp mic is common and will thin the low end.
