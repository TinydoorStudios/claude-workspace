# Genre Profiles — sonic signature → EQ influence

Step 4 detail. Each profile: what the style should sound like, how aggressive to be, and the hard
rules. The spine is the Genre Modifiers in `eq-starting-points.md`; this expands it to the genres
Brian actually works. Treat these as the *bend* you apply to the sourced baseline — not standalone
recipes.

**Aggressiveness scale** (Brian's default leans aggressive because of reverberant/outdoor rooms):
`Minimal → Conservative → Moderate → Aggressive`.

---

## Acoustic-forward family — lighter hand, let the mic work

### Classical (live reinforcement)
- **Sound:** natural, blended, the room is part of the instrument.
- **Aggressiveness:** Minimal. Cuts only, small amounts.
- **Rules:** trust placement; EQ is correction, not shaping. No aggressive moves anywhere. Don't
  touch a designed-in lift (e.g. the DPA 4099's 10–12 kHz boost).

### Classical recording (not reinforcement)
- **Sound:** faithful capture.
- **Aggressiveness:** Minimal / correction-only across the board.
- **Rules:** the mic placement is doing the work. Trust what you hear. Gentle HPF at most.

### Chamber / orchestral
- **Sound:** sectional balance, natural air.
- **Aggressiveness:** Minimal–Conservative.
- **Rules:** spot mics blend under the mains; don't let a spot stick out. Low-mid buildup from close
  spots is the main thing to manage.

### Celtic / Irish trad
- **Sound:** warmth and body, sustained drones and bowed/blown notes.
- **Aggressiveness:** Conservative.
- **Rules:** **5 ms+ attack on dynamics; never gate sustained notes.** Bodhrán is the high-risk
  channel at Memo (200 Hz). Uilleann pipes: chanter brightness vs. bag/bellows noise, wide dynamic
  range → compression helps. Snare wants body over crack.

### Folk / acoustic / singer-songwriter
- **Sound:** intimate, natural, vocal-forward.
- **Aggressiveness:** Conservative.
- **Rules:** **piezo/pickup quack at 1.5–2 kHz is the primary cut** on any acoustic DI/pickup.
  Proximity on close-miked acoustic; preserve body. Vocals still cuts-only.

### Jazz (small ensemble)
- **Sound:** natural, dynamic, unforced.
- **Aggressiveness:** Conservative–Moderate (treat a quiet trio like acoustic-forward).
- **Rules:** kick natural (thump-not-click), gentle everything, ride the dynamics. At Memo, small
  jazz = acoustic-forward rules.

### Bluegrass / old-time
- **Sound:** woody, present, often single-mic or minimal-mic.
- **Aggressiveness:** Conservative.
- **Rules:** banjo/mando upper-mid presence vs. harshness; upright bass fundamental vs. boom; watch
  feedback on the single-mic blend. Natural over hyped.

## Mid-aggression family

### Blues / soul
- **Sound:** thick, warm, vocal and guitar forward.
- **Aggressiveness:** Moderate.
- **Rules:** more **thump** on the kick; keep low-mid body on guitars; don't over-scoop. Horn
  sections want warmth with controlled upper-mid bite.

### Salsa / Latin / Afro-Cuban
- **Sound:** bright, punchy percussion, tight low end, horns cut through.
- **Aggressiveness:** Moderate–Aggressive (lots of open mics, busy stage).
- **Rules:** timbales/bongó/conga each need their own low-mid management to avoid a percussion mud
  pile; piano/keys carve against horns; clave and bell must read. (Brian's FSQ Salsa show is the
  reference layout.) Many open mics → cut-heavy, feedback-aware.

### R&B / funk
- **Sound:** tight, punchy, defined low end, slap and snap.
- **Aggressiveness:** Moderate–Aggressive.
- **Rules:** kick + bass separation (carve so they don't share 80–120 Hz); snap on snare; keep
  guitars/keys out of the vocal's way.

---

## Dense / loud family — deeper cuts for separation

### Rock
- **Sound:** big, aggressive, **click** on the kick, present guitars.
- **Aggressiveness:** Aggressive.
- **Rules:** more click than thump; scoop guitar mud for separation; tight gates where they help;
  vocals fight a loud stage → aggressive cuts-only and decisive HPF.

### Gospel & choir / large vocal groups
- **Sound:** big, dense, lots of voices and a full band.
- **Aggressiveness:** Aggressive for separation; feedback headroom is tight.
- **Rules:** **massed-voice upper-mid nasality** is the first cut on choir mics. Choir gate usually
  off (the group never goes fully silent). Solo vocals cuts-only, aggressive. Snare crack to cut
  through. Many open choir mics = the feedback risk is real — be disciplined.

### Pop / Top-40 / cover bands
- **Sound:** polished, modern, everything present.
- **Aggressiveness:** Moderate–Aggressive depending on stage volume.
- **Rules:** vocal intelligibility is king (cuts-only); kick/bass tight; carve space deliberately.

---

## When the genre is a hybrid

Big-band jazz with a wall of brass is *dense/loud*, not acoustic-forward — treat it like a dense mix
even though it's "jazz." A stripped acoustic set inside a rock show flips the other way. If the label
and the reality diverge, go by the reality, and if you're unsure which way it leans, **ask** — this
choice moves the whole aggressiveness scale and is exactly the kind of fork worth a pause.

## Vocals are the constant

On **every** genre above, vocals are **cuts-only**. That never bends. It's feedback control, not a
stylistic choice. The genre only changes how aggressive the cuts are and where the problem sits, not
the cuts-only rule.
