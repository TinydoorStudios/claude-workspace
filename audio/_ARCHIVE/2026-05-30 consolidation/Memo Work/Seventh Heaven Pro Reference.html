CORE PRINCIPLE — MEMO RT60

The Memo adds roughly 1.6 seconds of natural decay in a working show. Factory preset decay times are designed for drier rooms. For most shows at Memo, pull decay time down 30–40% from factory as a starting point. Let the room do the late field work. You're adding placement and character — not competing with the room.

Exception: Very close-mic'd sources (DI bass, kick inside mic, lip-close vocal) that pick up minimal natural room need reverb for placement context — these can run longer because the mic isn't picking up the room.
CONTROLS REFERENCE
Decay Time

RT60 in seconds. Right-click "Decay Time" to snap to exact sampled M7 values — interpolation between those points is fine, but at the sampled values you're hearing the actual hardware capture. Factory values are in the spec tables below.

Pre-Delay

Affects late reverb and VLF only. Does not move the early reflections (that's an M7 design behavior). Range 0–500 ms; tempo-sync available. Minimum 10 ms on most sends; 20–30 ms on vocals.

VLF (Very Low Frequency Reverb)

A single level slider — not an EQ band. Controls the level of the M7's dedicated sub-200 Hz reverb component: body and fullness, metered separately. Factory VLF levels from presets PDF range from 0 dB to −20 dB relative to the late tail.

At Memo, the room reinforces 60–315 Hz through standing waves — cut VLF aggressively:

Classical / acoustic−18 to −20 dB, or off entirely
Jazz / Celtic / folk−15 to −18 dB

Early / Late (Balance Slider)
⚠ Critical Behavior — Hard Jump to MAX
The slider range is −20 dB to Equal Mix. Crossing Equal Mix causes an immediate hard jump to MAX on the opposite side — there is no gradual crossfade through center. In practice:

• Slider in the Early half: Early signal = displayed dB value, Late = MAX
• Slider in the Late half: Late signal = displayed dB value, Early = MAX
• Equal Mix = the only balanced point

Example: "Early: −15 dB, Late: MAX" means the slider is positioned in the Early half at −15 dB.

Push toward Early for room placement without long tail; push toward Late for bloom and wash. At Memo, most sends benefit from slightly more Early than factory — the room handles the late field.

Notation standard: Use "Early: −X dB, Late: MAX" or "Equal Mix" — never percentage pairs (35/65, 60/40, etc.).

Preset: Halls 1 / Boston Hall A | Decay 1.3s • PreDelay 20ms • VLF −18dB • Early: −8 dB, Late: MAX • Late Rolloff 5kHz • Mix 18% — classical vocal
Early Reflections Set (0–31)

Thirty-two discrete early reflection patterns sampled from the M7. Lower numbers = small spaces; higher = large spaces. More reflective environments within each group have higher numbers. Factory value shown in spec tables.

Early Rolloff / Late Rolloff

Two separate filter cutpoint controls. Not called "HF Damp" anywhere in the plugin.

ControlMemo Target by Genre
Late RolloffClassical/acoustic: 4–6 kHz · Jazz/folk: 6–9 kHz · Contemporary: 8–10 kHz
Early RolloffUsually leave at factory
Frequency Dependent Decay Time

HF and LF multipliers relative to main RT60. At Memo with low-end buildup, an LF multiplier below 1.0 tightens bass reverb time without touching main decay.

Ducker

Two modes: Reverb (late tail + VLF only) or Wet Mix (all signals). For vocals, use Reverb mode — early reflections stay up for placement while tail ducks during phrases. Controls: Threshold, Ratio, Release, Trim.

Algorithm Types
TypeCharacterWhen to Use
V1Static, realistic, low modulation — "pure" Bricasti soundClassical, jazz, Celtic, acoustic folk, chamber. Source keeps its character.
V2Brighter, more modulated, lush bloomGospel, contemporary, pop/rock, dense choir. Wash and bloom over accuracy.
NLNon-decaying initial region (~50–300ms) then ~200ms exponential tailDrums only, gated effects. Not a primary tool at Memo.

When in doubt on acoustic shows: V1. When in doubt on contemporary: V2 for plates/vocals, V1 for halls.

LAYERING STRATEGY

Classic two-reverb move: short chamber/plate for close perspective + long hall pre-delayed 40–80 ms for tail. At Memo, the room itself is the third layer — most useful on close-mic'd sources that aren't picking up much natural room.

Example — vocal chain: Chambers 1 / Vocal Chamber (1.0s, 10ms pre-delay) + Halls 1 / Boston Hall A (1.3s, 50ms pre-delay). Both VLF at −18dB+. The Vocal Chamber provides sense of place; the Boston Hall adds size between phrases. The room handles the diffuse late field naturally.

Underhat / underhead offset: When cymbal mics are in underhat/underhead position, add +2 to +3 dB to the reverb send to compensate for reduced natural air. Preset choice doesn't change — only the send level.
