### 4.2.1 Channel Macros (Q8 Only)

![Q8 Specific Features (manual p.207)](/figures/ref-p207-1.png)

Above every channel LCD on the three lower sections of the console, there are two buttons labelled ‘a’ and ‘b’.

These trigger channel macros, which are macros that can be triggered from specific channels. To create a channel macro, go to Master Screen > Setup > Channel Macros. This will open the ‘Channel Macro Editor’.

Add a new channel macro from the top left corner of the panel and use the list of commands in the middle to define the functionality of the channel macro. This can also be done by using the ‘capture’ command in the top right corner of the panel. On a new channel macro, toggle ‘capture’, manually carry out the process that the channel macro is meant to trigger, then toggle ‘capture’ again to complete the process. Make sure that the range of channels that the channel macro effects is correct before assigning it to a channel.

To assign a channel macro, select the desired channel, then tap one of the black rectangles labelled ‘a’, ‘b’, ‘c’, and ‘d’ in the Channel Macro Editor panel. These represent the available slots for channel macro assignment. The selected slot will turn the colour of the assigned channel macro and will display its name. Tapping the same slot again will unassign the channel macro. It is possible to assign the same channel macro to all four slots on a single channel, and to slots on different channels. The name of the selected channel is displayed clearly in the middle of the Channel Macro Editor panel.

There are two ways to trigger channel macros. The worksurface buttons above channel LCDs will trigger any channel macros assigned to slots ‘a’ and ‘b’ on that channel and are thus labelled. To trigger channel macros in slots ‘c’ and ‘d’, select ‘Macros’ from the display options in the top left corner of the screen. This will change the meter view to a channel macro view, where the channel macros in all four slots can be triggered by touch.

Note: always ensure the correct channel is selected before assigning a channel macro.

### 4.2.2 Expanded Channel Control Area (Q8 Only)

The Expanded Channel Control Area, positioned between the channel strip and the channel meters, can be used to view and adjust various processing controls for any selected channel. If no channel is selected, it will appear blank, only displaying the DiGiCo logo. Once a channel has been selected, the buttons along the left- hand side of the expanded channel control area can be used to select different ‘views’. These are:

‘Proc’, which displays input processing controls such as gain, trim, DiGiTube, Delay, and Stereo width.

‘EQ’, which displays parametric EQ and Dynamic EQ controls.

‘Dyn’ which displays dynamics such as comp, gate, or ducker.

‘KLANG’ which displays KLANG controls when an aux send is selected.

‘Aux’ which displays controls for Aux send nodes.

At the top of each of these views, there are icons to turn controls on and off, copy processing values, and open the presets panel.

A scrollable list of auxes is displayed at the top of the Expanded Channel Control Area. If there are more than twenty-four auxes, an ‘Expand’ button appears on the left-hand side. Pressing it will expand the aux list to occupy the entire Expanded Channel Control Area.

To access the Expanded Channel Control Area on either the centre upper or centre lower sections, select a channel on either section, then press ‘Select’ in the top left area of the channel view. Pressing ‘Bank’ will toggle back to the channel strip view.

The information displayed in the Expanded Channel Control Area is different based on the type of channel. For instance, output channels will have the option on the right-hand side of their ‘EQ’ view to control bands 1-4 or 5-8, and stereo inputs will have additional stereo processing controls in their ‘Proc’ view.

On the left-hand side of the Expanded Channel Control Area, there are two side-by-side buttons labelled ‘SD’ and ‘M’. These can be used to toggle between SD processing, and Mustard processing. When ‘M’, or Mustard, is selected, the top and bottom of the Expanded Channel Control Area will turn yellow to denote that Mustard processing is being viewed. In this instance, ‘Proc’ view will display Mustard Tube controls, ‘EQ’ will display Mustard EQ controls, and ‘Dyn’ will display Mustard dynamics.

The Expanded Channel Control Area can also be used to display Nodal Processing EQ and Dynamics. To do this, select an aux with nodal processing active from the list at the top of the Expanded Channel Control Area. The top and bottom of the Expanded Channel Control Area will turn purple to denote that nodal processing is being viewed. ‘EQ’ view will display nodal EQ, and ‘Dyn’ will display nodal Dynamics. To exit nodal processing view, select either ‘SD’ or ‘M’ from the left-hand side of the Expanded Channel Control Area.

### 4.2.3 The Master Faders (Q8 Only)

By default, the left-hand faders in the centre of the worksurface are assigned to the Master buss, which is the lowest stereo group output by default.

Also, by default, the right-hand faders are assigned to Matrix 1 and Matrix 2.

In addition, these faders can be reassigned to control any input channel or buss of your choice using the

**Layout>Fader Banks panel**

Press the Assign L1/L2 or Assign R1/R2 buttons and the Channel List will be displayed.

Select a channel by touching its entry in the Channel List and it will be assigned to the relevant Master Fader. These faders can also be unassigned (set to empty) by pressing the Unassign L1/L2 or Unassign R1/R2 buttons

### 4.2.4 Macroders (Q8 Only)

On every worksurface section, there is a set of four encoders and buttons around an LCD screen under the Dynamics controls. These are macroders, which can be assigned to control any parameter for a selected channel. By default, they are assigned to control aux send nodes 1 to 4.

![Q8 Specific Features (manual p.213)](/figures/ref-p213-1.png)

![Q8 Specific Features (manual p.213)](/figures/ref-p213-2.png)

Macroder functions and controls can be found in the ‘Macroder Editor’ panel, which can be accessed by going to Master Screen > Setup > Macroders. In the top left corner of the panel, there are three buttons labelled ‘New’, which adds a new macroder, ‘Duplicate’, which duplicates the selected macroder, and ‘Delete’ which deletes one or multiple macroders. The list of commands in the middle to define the functionality of the macroder.

This can also be done by using the ‘capture’ command in the top right corner of the panel. On a new macroder, toggle ‘capture’, manually change the parameters that the macroder is intended to control, then toggle ‘capture’ again to complete the process. At the bottom of the Macroder Editor panel, assign the macroder to one of the worksurface encoders, which are represented by little circles in the same formation as the encoders on the worksurface. Tapping once will assign the macroder to the encoder, and tapping again will unassign it, and leave the encoder blank. Once assigned, the appropriate worksurface encoder will have the colour of the LED ring around it change to match the parameter it affects, and the name of the macroder will appear on the LCD.

Macroders can also be assigned to specific channels, as opposed to any channel on a section of the worksurface. To do this, toggle ‘Assign to Channel’ in the bottom left corner of the Macroder Editor panel. This will open a little window that displays the name of the last selected channel, and a schematic of one set of four worksurface macroder encoders. Simply tapping on any one of the four encoders will assign the macroder to the selected channel only. That way, whatever section the channel is on, selecting it will change the assignment of the macroder encoders on that section to match whatever was particularly assigned.

Note that Macroders are fundamentally different from Macros and Channel Macros in that they don’t instantly trigger a set of commands. Instead, they control parameter values as the encoders are turned, much like underscreen rotaries.

### 4.2.6 Layers and Banks (Q8 Only)

The QUANTUM 8's worksurface is divided into Layers and Banks. Each Bank contains twelve channels, and the channels which are currently active on the control surface are defined using the bank (left column) and layer (right column) buttons on the switcher screen to the right of the channel strip section’s faders.

The view in the switcher screen can be locked to prevent accidental changes by holding down on the button in the top left corner of the screen.

It can also be scrolled by swiping to the right to display Snapshot previous and Next buttons. The swipe function can also be locked by touching the button in the top right corner of the screen.

![Q8 Specific Features (manual p.215)](/figures/ref-p215-2.png)

![Q8 Specific Features (manual p.215)](/figures/ref-p215-3.png)

![Q8 Specific Features (manual p.215)](/figures/ref-p215-4.png)

A ‘bank’ is a set of twelve faders, and a ‘layer’ contains up to six ‘banks’. There are up to six ‘layers’ on the left, centre lower, and right sections of the desk, and four ‘banks’ on the centre upper section.

Tapping a bank (left column) button on the Switcher screen assigns the channels in that bank to the surface. To access a bank of faders within another layer, tapping the appropriate layer (right column) button on the Switcher screen will assign the banks in that layer to the ‘banks’ column on the Switcher screen. Holding any bank or layer button down for a couple of seconds will switch the left and right worksurface sections to the same bank level or layer.

NOTE: There is also a Master Screen Assign button next to each set of faders on the centre section which is used to switch the centre section to display the Master Screen.

The specific channels which are contained within each Bank are defined in the Layout > Fader Banks panel. By default, the Input channels will be assigned to Layer 1 on the left and right sections of the console. The different output channels and Control Groups will be assigned to the banks on the centre sections. These bank assignments can be customised by the user in the Fader Banks panel and saved in a session at any time.
